A · Digital rouble (Aksakov)

Federal Law 340-FZ of 24.07.2023 — bills 270838-8 and 270852-8

The digital rouble was introduced into law by two linked instruments of 24 July 2023: Federal Law 340-FZ "On Amendments to Certain Legislative Acts of the Russian Federation" (bill No. 270838-8) and the accompanying Federal Law 339-FZ amending the Civil Code (bill No. 270852-8); the texts of both instruments are published on publication.pravo.gov.ru and kremlin.ru/acts/bank/49579. The SOZD card at sozd.duma.gov.ru/bill/270838-8 lists 12 co-authors; the key figure among them is A. G. Aksakov, chair of the State Duma Committee on Financial Markets since 2016 and a member of the SRZP faction; other co-authors include Klishas (Federation Council) and Svistunov (LDPR).

Anatoly Gennadyevich Aksakov (Анатолий Геннадьевич Аксаков) (born 28.11.1957) — a State Duma deputy since 1999, serving 25 consecutive years; chair of the board of the Association of Russian Banks since 2006; member of the RSPP executive board. According to declarator.org/person/33 — 2024 declaration: income approximately 14.4 million ₽, real estate 1,262 m². Aksakov is the public face of lobbying for CBDC (Central Bank Digital Currency) in Russia and has for many years served as the coordination node between the Central Bank, the banking sector, and the Duma.

The third-reading vote on bill 270838-8 took place on 11 July 2023. vote_id 123266 (raw XML): 385 for, 0 against, 1 abstained; CPRF 48/0/1/8, LDPR 14/0/0/8, SRZP 19/0/0/9, NL 11/0/0/4. The vote of 13.07.2023 (part 2) — vote_id 123339: 391/0/0, unanimous among those voting. The sole abstention in the first vote was Kornienko (CPRF).

The technical properties of the digital rouble (per the official concept of the Bank of Russia): the Central Bank of Russia is both the platform operator and issuer of this third form of the national currency; all transactions are technically visible to the Bank of Russia in real time; account freezing by regulatory decision without a court order is technically possible; 'earmarked' budget roubles — the technical ability to restrict purchases to certain categories of goods for recipients of state funds. The digital rouble thus structurally equips the state with infrastructure to monitor every purchase by every pensioner and every public-sector employee — that is, the SRZP electorate. On the overlap of this infrastructure with other digital controls (RKN, MAX, the Unified Biometric System (EBS)) — see the summary page Digital Restrictions. This is a structural case of a party (SRZP) producing the infrastructure of financial control over its own electorate.

B · Foreign agents (Lugovoy)

Federal Law 255-FZ of 14.07.2022 — bill 113045-8

The unified foreign-agent law — Federal Law 255-FZ of 14.07.2022 "On the Monitoring of the Activities of Persons Under Foreign Influence" (bill No. 113045-8; text on pravo.gov.ru). The law consolidated four previous foreign-agent laws (media-2012, NGOs-2017, individuals-2019, media-2020) into a single code and substantially expanded the category of persons covered: henceforth a 'foreign agent' may be designated not only on the basis of foreign funding but also on the basis of being "under foreign influence" — a formulation broad enough to cover virtually any public activity disapproved of by the regulator.

The SOZD card at sozd.duma.gov.ru/bill/113045-8 lists 18 co-authors. Among them are co-authors from three 'opposition' factions (out of four): A. K. Lugovoy (LDPR), S. D. Leonov (LDPR), A. M. Babakov (SRZP), M. G. Delyagin (SRZP), Ya. V. Lantratova (SRZP), G. P. Chemeris (NL), A. O. Tkachyov (NL) — plus V. I. Piskarev (UR) as chair of the Security Committee and others. A law formally identified with United Russia was in reality co-signed by the upper echelons of the LDPR, SRZP, and New People factions — precisely those factions that publicly position themselves as defenders of 'ordinary Russian people' against state pressure.

Andrei Konstantinovich Lugovoy (Андрей Константинович Луговой) (born 19.09.1966) — a State Duma deputy for the LDPR since 2007 and deputy chair of the Duma Committee on Security and Counter-Corruption. A former officer of the 1st Department of the Federal Protective Service (FSO) from 1991 to 1996; in the 1990s he provided personal protection to Ye. Gaidar, S. Filatov, A. Kozyrev, and B. Berezovsky. After leaving state service he moved into private security work in Berezovsky's circle, then into independent business and a political career via the LDPR from 2007.

Third-reading vote 29.06.2022 — vote_id 118599 (raw XML): 331 for, 5 against, 11 abstained. Factional breakdown: UR — an overwhelming majority for; SRZP 24/0/0/4 (24 for, the rest absent); LDPR 20/0/0/3; NL 3/0/10/2 (10 abstentions — an anomaly, the only instance of mass abstention within the New People faction on a structurally significant vote); CPRF 0/4/1/51 — four against, one abstention, and mass absence of 51 deputies out of 57: the CPRF faction de facto avoided voting through tactical non-attendance rather than a public 'against'. On the structural functions of the LDPR — see the LDPR dossier.

C · The Litvinenko parallel

Lugovoy and polonium-210 — between the murder of 2006 and the law of 2022

On 23 November 2006 in London, at University College Hospital, Alexander Valterovich Litvinenko died — a former lieutenant-colonel of Russia's FSB who had been granted political asylum in the United Kingdom in 2000. The cause of death was acute radiation syndrome resulting from poisoning with polonium-210; the lethal dose was received on 1 November 2006 in the Pine Bar of the Millennium Hotel on Grosvenor Square. Before his death, Litvinenko publicly accused Vladimir Putin of ordering his poisoning, directing the accusation to the President personally through the journalist Alex Goldfarb.

On 21 January 2016, Sir Robert Owen (Роберт Оуэн), chairman of the public inquiry into the Litvinenko case, published the final report running to more than 300 pages (gov.uk/government/publications/the-litvinenko-inquiry-report). The report's key finding: "I am sure that Mr Lugovoy and Mr Kovtun placed the polonium-210 in the teapot at the Pine Bar on 1 November 2006, with the intention of poisoning Mr Litvinenko." The report further states that the operation was "probably approved" by FSB director N. P. Patrushev and President V. V. Putin.

On the same day — 21 January 2016 — HM Treasury published a notice applying an asset-freezing regime to Lugovoy and Kovtun (uk-sanctions-litvinenko.csv). The British sanctions were renewed in 2018, 2020, and 2022 — each time without any reduction in scope.

In January 2017, the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) added A. K. Lugovoy to the SDN List (Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List) under the Magnitsky Act, directed at persons implicated in human-rights violations (lugovoy-extract.csv). From that point on Lugovoy is simultaneously a sitting deputy of the State Duma of the Russian Federation, a subject of sanctions by two G7 states (United Kingdom, United States), and a public co-author of laws on censorship and digital control in Russia.

Sixteen years separate the murder of Litvinenko (November 2006) from Lugovoy's co-authorship of Federal Law 255-FZ on the unified foreign-agent law (July 2022). The parallel is structural: a person who, according to the findings of a British public inquiry, carried out a state killing in London, in Moscow becomes one of the principal co-authors of a law that converts the public activity of Russian citizens — journalists, musicians, lawyers, vets — into "activity under foreign influence" with serious legal consequences. This is not a rhetorical device but a documented coincidence of biography and public legislative role.

D · The foreign-agent campaign

From 0 in 2017 to ≥1,100 persons on the Ministry of Justice register as of 30.04.2026

The 2012 law on 'foreign-agent' media outlets created a register that initially contained only a handful of entries. The extensions of 2017 (media foreign agents), 2019 (individuals), 2020 (media), and then the unified Federal Law 255-FZ of 14.07.2022 — each step expanded both the range of persons covered and the toolkit of control. As of 30 April 2026, the Ministry of Justice foreign-agent register contains ≥1,100 persons: journalists, vets, musicians, psychologists, lawyers, mothers of large families, authors of children's books (minjust.gov.ru). The actual scope of application of the law is dozens of times broader than the stated goal — protection from "foreign interference": the register has been extended to any public activity the regulator finds inconvenient.

Following Federal Law 255-FZ came two packages of additional restrictions in which Lugovoy again appears as co-author: Federal Law 99-FZ of May 2024 — a ban on foreign agents participating in elections at any level (standing as candidates, campaigning, or funding campaigns); and Federal Law 414-FZ of December 2024 — an obligation for foreign agents to hold all fees and income in special rouble accounts at banks with mandatory reporting to the Ministry of Justice. These two packages close the two remaining possibilities for persons on the register — electoral participation and free disposal of their own money.

Among those placed on the register after Federal Law 255-FZ came into force are Lev Markovich Shlosberg (from 16.06.2023), leader of Pskov Yabloko; Boris Lazarevich Vishnevsky (from 29.03.2024), a deputy of the St Petersburg Legislative Assembly for Yabloko; and nine or more other Yabloko members at various points in 2022–2026. Yabloko is a formally registered parliamentary party (registered by the Ministry of Justice, with regional structures) that regularly contests elections — and simultaneously the only party whose sitting deputies and leaders are being mass-designated as foreign agents by the Ministry of Justice.

The structural point: a law written by co-authors from three 'opposition' factions (SRZP, LDPR, New People) is applied primarily not against foreign spies and not against the systemic opposition those factions nominally represent, but against journalists, activists, and members of the non-parliamentary party Yabloko — that is, against the genuinely non-systemic opposition, on whose electoral terrain the parliamentary 'opposition' parties were formally supposed to be competing. Co-authorship of Federal Law 255-FZ is the public infrastructure through which SRZP, LDPR, and New People clear their own electoral field.
