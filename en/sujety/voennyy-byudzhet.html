Defence Budget — 32.5% of the treasury.

The 2025 federal budget (Bill No. 727320-8, enacted as Federal Law No. 419-FZ of 30.11.2024) allocates approximately 13.5 trillion roubles — 32.5% of total expenditure — to 'National Defence': a post-Soviet record and 3.3–3.4 times the combined spending on healthcare and education. Third-reading vote 21.11.2024: CPRF and A Just Russia — For Truth (SRZP) abstained as blocs; LDPR and New People voted in favour. However, a formal 'not in favour' is functionally void: blocking requires ≥226 votes, while the combined opposition holds ≤90. United Russia's constitutional majority (325 of 450 mandates) secures the passage of any bill without opposition participation.

A · Origins of FZ-419

How the 2025–2027 federal budget was passed

The draft federal budget for 2025 and the planning period 2026–2027 was submitted by the Government of the Russian Federation to the State Duma on 30.09.2024 as Bill No. 727320-8. The first reading took place on 24.10.2024, the second on 14.11.2024, and the third on 21.11.2024. The Federation Council approved the law on 27.11.2024; the President signed it on 30.11.2024, and it was published as Federal Law No. 419-FZ of 30.11.2024. The principal expenditure headings for 2025: 'National Defence' — ≈13.5 trillion roubles (32.5% of total expenditure); healthcare — 1.86 trillion roubles; education — 1.58 trillion roubles; social policy — approximately 6.5 trillion roubles (Ministry of Finance of the Russian Federation, РБК).

The share of defence spending in the federal budget has been rising systematically since 2014: approximately 17% in 2013, 16.5% in 2021, 28.3% in 2024, and 32.5% in 2025. As a share of GDP, this represents growth from 3.4% in 2013 to 6.3% in 2024 and an estimated 6.7–8.7% in 2025 (depending on the methodology, including the treatment of classified items). This is the highest share among G20 countries and one of the highest in the world — comparable to military-administrative regimes on a wartime footing, not modern peacetime economies (Коммерсантъ, Ведомости).

The structural priority is self-evident from the figures: 'National Defence' — 13.5 trillion roubles; healthcare — 1.86 trillion roubles; education — 1.58 trillion roubles. The ratios are direct: defence is 7.3 times larger than healthcare and 8.5 times larger than education; combined healthcare and education (3.44 trillion roubles) amounts to less than a quarter of the defence line. Case No. 5 in 09-betrayal-cases.md cites the softer ratio of '3.3–3.4 times' (likely calculated against the aggregate of all social expenditure including social policy); whichever methodology is applied, the structural priority of defence over social spending is embedded in the law itself.

For full context — a parallel with the preceding budget cycle. The 2024–2026 budget (Bill No. 448554-8, enacted as Federal Law No. 540-FZ of 27.11.2023) was passed at third reading on 17.11.2023. In that vote, the CPRF and SRZP factions voted 'against' as blocs, whereas in 2024 they shifted to abstaining. Structurally, this is a softening of the opposition position against a backdrop of doubled defence spending: the higher the share of defence expenditure in the budget, the milder the formal opposition stance.

B · Faction vote

How the 5 factions voted on 21.11.2024

On 21.11.2024 a plenary session of the State Duma (Государственная Дума) held the third reading of Bill No. 727320-8. The roll-call vote is available at vote.duma.gov.ru; no local XML copy of this vote is stored in the repository — links to the online source are provided. Faction breakdown: United Russia (UR) — voted in favour as a bloc; CPRF — abstained as a bloc; LDPR — voted in favour; A Just Russia — For Truth (SRZP) — abstained as a bloc; New People — voted in favour. The figures below are approximations based on faction composition in November 2024; exact present/absent deputy counts should be verified against the raw XML at the live URL vote.duma.gov.ru.

Structural result of the vote: approximately 350 deputies voted in favour (UR + LDPR + New People); abstentions — approximately 69 deputies (CPRF + SRZP). No faction registered votes against. This is the key distinction of the 2024 episode from the 2024–2026 budget (17.11.2023), in which CPRF and SRZP formally voted 'against': against a backdrop of doubled defence spending, the opposition shifted from 'against' to 'abstain' — a softer position.

The key analytical observation: none of the 'opposition' factions put to a separate vote any amendment to reduce defence spending — neither in 2023 nor in 2024. The State Duma's plenary transcripts contain no publicly documented instance of such an amendment being tabled. A formal position of 'abstained' or 'voted against the budget as a whole' is not accompanied by any procedural action aimed specifically at blocking growth in the 'National Defence' heading. This structurally distinguishes the 'defence budget 2025' case from a situation in which the opposition genuinely attempted to halt or constrain the expansion of the 'National Defence' line.

Detailed context for each faction is on the party pages: United Russia, CPRF, LDPR, SRZP, New People.

C · Majority arithmetic

Why 'against' means nothing in this Duma

The State Duma has 450 deputies. Different decisions require different vote thresholds: a simple majority — 226 votes (for most federal laws, including the budget); a constitutional majority — 300 (for federal constitutional laws and constitutional amendments, except Chapters 1, 2, and 9); 338 votes to override a presidential or Federation Council veto. This is the structural framework within which every vote takes place.

Faction composition of the 8th convocation (2021 elections, adjusted for subsequent changes): United Russia — 325 mandates (324 formal seats plus two independents who joined the faction), CPRF — 57, LDPR — 23, SRZP — 27, New People — 15, remainder — independents. This means that United Russia alone holds a constitutional majority (325 > 300) and can pass any legislation, including constitutional amendments (except those touching the chapters on the federal structure and fundamental rights). The combined opposition CPRF + SRZP = ≤90 mandates; together with LDPR and New People — approximately 122. No arithmetical combination of opposition factions reaches the blocking threshold of 226. For more on this arithmetic, see the 'Majority Ladder' on the elections page.

Structural conclusion. Voting 'against' in a system with one party's constitutional majority is a symbolic act, not an obstacle. It does not change the outcome, does not delay passage of a law by a single day, and does not force the ruling bloc to compromise on individual budget lines. In this sense, a formal 'vote against the budget' by the CPRF or SRZP is a public gesture directed at their own electorate, not a procedural action aimed at the content of the law. Real opposition would require either all opposition factions uniting to reach the threshold of 226 (arithmetically impossible) or the tabling and public defence of specific amendments to reduce defence spending — which, as the transcripts show, does not happen.

D · Alternatives

What would happen with a real opposition

In G20 countries, the share of defence spending in the federal budget typically does not exceed 5–12%. For comparison: the United States — approximately 13% of the federal budget (2024), the United Kingdom — approximately 6%, France — approximately 5%, Germany — approximately 4%. 32.5% is a separate regime, characteristic of war economies: North Korea, Iran during wartime, the USSR during 1979–1989. The 32.5% figure is itself an indicator that the 2025 budget is structurally not a peacetime budget but a wartime one. In a system with a genuine opposition, such a budget would be subject to extended public debate, amendments, bargaining over individual lines, and potentially outright rejection.

The trajectory of defence spending as a share of Russia's federal budget is instructive: 2013 — approximately 17%; 2014 (annexation of Crimea) — approximately 19%; 2018 — approximately 20%; 2021 — 16.5%; 2022 — 17.8%; 2023 — 21.2%; 2024 — 28.3%; 2025 — 32.5%. That is, a doubling over three years (2022 → 2025) and an almost doubling relative to 2013. At each stage, the 'opposition' in the Duma either voted in favour (budgets 2014–2022) or 'against/abstained' without procedural blocking — structurally, the budget was adopted in the Government's original draft in full.

The electoral base of CPRF and SRZP — pensioners, public-sector employees, provincial workers — is structurally harmed by inflation (10%+ in 2024–2025), real wage falls, and the closure of schools and hospitals ('optimisation'). These parties declare 'pension indexation', 'real wages', 'a social budget' — but do not block the structural priority of defence spending over social spending. Yet, according to the consolidated table of party budget financing, the CPRF receives approximately 76–84% of its income from the same federal budget against which it formally votes (see the CPRF party page; overview in 09-betrayal-cases.md). This is not a 'declarative contradiction' but a structural conflict of interest: the parties are dependent on the same budget they formally contest.
