● Cross-cutting theme 2024—2025 3 liquidations in 12 months

Three liquidations — 12 months.

From late 2024 to late 2025, Russia's Supreme Court liquidated three non-parliamentary parties: 'Party of Business' (Babkin; 27.11.2024), 'Civic Initiative' (Nechayev; June 2025) and 'Party of Growth' (Titov; 20.11.2025). All three liquidations formally rest on technical grounds — regional network below the minimum, non-participation in elections. Yet by timing, each liquidation follows a specific political event: the liquidation of 'Party of Business' — after Babkin's intensified lobbying and his move to LDPR; of 'Civic Initiative' — after the Nadezhdin 2024 campaign; of 'Party of Growth' — after the merger with New People. No direct causal link can be established from public documents, but the synchronisation is an indicator of a systemic pattern.

A · Context of the liquidations

Russian party legislation and its application in 2024—2025

After 2022 — a general tightening of requirements for party registration and oversight of political associations. The basic statutory requirements are set out in Federal Law No. 95-FZ of 11.07.2001 'On Political Parties' (with numerous amendments): a party must maintain regional branches in at least half of the constituent entities of the Russian Federation with a minimum membership; must participate in elections at least once every seven years; and must submit annual reports to the Ministry of Justice on its activities.

Russia's Supreme Court may liquidate a political party on a petition by the Ministry of Justice on one of three grounds: failure to meet regional-network requirements (branch count below the minimum or violation of membership requirements); non-participation in elections for seven consecutive years; political activity in violation of the law (this last ground is applied extremely rarely and requires substantial evidence). In practice, in 2024—2025 all three liquidations examined here were brought on the first two — technical — grounds; the political ground was not invoked.

Drawing on the source 02-cross-cutting/08-extra-parliamentary-liberals.md, one can identify four life trajectories for parties outside United Russia: (1) merger with a systemic party ('Party of Growth' → New People; Babkin → LDPR); (2) backing a non-systemic candidate → liquidation ('Civic Initiative' → Nadezhdin → liquidation); (3) marginalisation (Civil Platform, RPFJ, Green Alternative); (4) minimal electoral activity with retention of legal status (Yabloko). The concentration of three liquidations in a 12-month window is an indicator of a shift in the application of trajectories (1) and (2).

B · Party of Business (27.11.2024)

Babkin, Programme 1432, and the move to LDPR after liquidation

Konstantin Anatolyevich Babkin (b. 04.06.1971) is a co-owner of JSC Rostselmash (Константин Анатольевич Бабкин), president of the Russian Association of Specialised Machinery and Equipment Manufacturers 'Rosspetsmash', and president of the industrial union 'New Commonwealth'. He is one of Russia's largest lobbyists in the agricultural-machinery sector; 'Party of Business' was founded in 2012 as a party infrastructure to advance a pro-industrial, protectionist agenda.

The principal instrument of Babkin's lobbying was 'Programme 1432' — Resolution of the Government of the Russian Federation No. 1432 of 27.12.2012 on subsidising agricultural-machinery manufacturers. Babkin publicly described the programme: "Programme 1432… we credit to our own account." According to available estimates, in 2014—2018 approximately 78% of subsidies under Programme 1432 (≈34 bn ₽) went to Rostselmash and the St Petersburg Tractor Plant — that is, to companies linked to the Babkin group. The party served as public justification for maintaining and expanding the programme.

On 22 April 2024 the Ministry of Justice initiated proceedings in the Supreme Court to suspend the activities of 'Party of Business' on the grounds of an inadequate regional network. On 27 November 2024 Russia's Supreme Court granted the Ministry of Justice petition and liquidated the party (RIA — sud-1986031652; local copy of materials). Formally — a technical violation; in substance — the final termination of Babkin's party infrastructure.

In December 2024 — immediately after the liquidation — K. A. Babkin joined LDPR and was included in its economic council and supreme council. This is the first of the documented cases in this theme of a conversion of a non-parliamentary actor into a systemic one through merger: Babkin's party resources — staff, regional network, public lobbying experience — transferred from an independent structure to the umbrella of a systemic parliamentary party. For more detail, see the dossiers on 'Party of Business' and LDPR.

C · Civic Initiative (06.2025)

Nechayev, Nadezhdin's nomination, and liquidation 16 months later

Andrei Alekseyevich Nechayev (b. 02.02.1953; not to be confused with A. G. Nechayev of New People — the Faberlic founder) is Russia's first Minister of the Economy (1992—1993) in Yegor Gaidar's (Е. Т. Гайдара) government, one of the co-authors of the early-1990s economic reforms; from 1993 to 2013 — president of the Russian Financial Corporation (RFC); since 2013 — chairman of the party 'Civic Initiative'.

The party was documentably used as a nominating structure for non-systemic candidates. Key cases: in 2018, 'Civic Initiative' nominated Ksenia Sobchak as a presidential candidate; in December 2023 an attempt was made to support Yekaterina Duntsova; in December 2023 the party nominated Boris Borisovich Nadezhdin as a presidential candidate for the 17 March 2024 election. The Nadezhdin campaign collected tens of thousands of signatures in queues across Russia and became the largest public expression of an anti-war position in electoral form in 2023—2024.

On 8 February 2024, Russia's CEC (Central Election Commission) refused Boris B. Nadezhdin's registration as a presidential candidate on the grounds that the share of 'defective' signatures in the sample checked was 9.3% — above the statutory threshold of 5%. The decision was based on a selective expert check and was challenged by Nadezhdin's campaign team without changing the final verdict. 16 months after the presidential election — in June 2025 — Russia's Supreme Court liquidated 'Civic Initiative' on a petition by the Ministry of Justice (local copy of materials); the stated formal ground was failure to meet regional-network requirements.

🔴 The sequence 'Nadezhdin nomination 2023 → CEC refusal 2024 → party liquidation 2025' is the subject of a well-founded analytical discussion. No direct causal link between Nadezhdin's nomination and the party's liquidation can be established from public documents: the Ministry of Justice petition formally rests on technical criteria. However, the three events form a sequential chronology and concern the same party structure; such a sequence is not a unique anomaly — it is the second documented case in our sample of a conversion of a non-parliamentary resource into an illegitimate one (after 'Party of Business'). For more detail, see the dossier on 'Civic Initiative'.

D · Party of Growth (20.11.2025)

Titov, merger with New People, and technical liquidation

Boris Yuryevich Titov (b. 24.12.1960) is a Russian entrepreneur and public official; from 22 June 2012 — Presidential Commissioner for Entrepreneurs' Rights (business ombudsman; Presidential Decree No. 879 of 22.06.2012, local copy); from 2024 — also Special Representative of the President of the Russian Federation for Liaison with International Organisations for Sustainable Development Goals (Decree No. 299/2024, local copy).

In commercial terms, Titov's principal asset is the Abrau-Durso group of companies (producers of sparkling and champagne-style wines), in which B. Yu. Titov (Б. Ю. Титов) through SVL Group controls ≈58% of shares; day-to-day management of the group has been led since 2012 by his son Pavel Borisovich Titov. This configuration — an active official with a dual presidential mandate simultaneously holding a controlling stake in a large business through a family circle — is characteristic of the 'systemic business ombudsmen' segment and does not belong to the politically disloyal part of the business community.

On 19 April 2024, 'Party of Growth' formally announced its merger with New People: the majority of staff and regional structures transferred to NL. On 20 November 2025 Russia's Supreme Court liquidated 'Party of Growth' on a petition by the Ministry of Justice (Forbes — 550320; local copy of materials); the formal ground — failure of the regional network to meet the minimum. In substance — a technical formality 19 months after the effective merger with NL; 'Party of Growth' was no longer an independent party structure at the time of liquidation. This is the first documented case in this theme of a conversion through merger with a systemic party followed by formal closure — following the same scenario applied to Babkin/'Party of Business'. For more detail, see the dossiers on 'Party of Growth' and New People.

E · Chronological maths

Three liquidations in a 12-month window — anomaly and four trajectories

The concentration of three non-parliamentary-party liquidations in a window of 12 months is a statistical anomaly against the preceding period. From 2012 (the year in which declaratory party registration was restored) to 2024, non-parliamentary-party liquidations proceeded at a conditional average of two to three parties per year, without a marked concentration in short time windows and without visible coincidence with specific political events. Three liquidations in the window November 2024 — November 2025 departs from that average.

For historical perspective — the parallel of PARNAS (People's Freedom Party; registered in 2012, former leaders — M. M. Kasyanov, B. Ye. Nemtsov, V. A. Ryzhkov). In the 2016 State Duma election, PARNAS received 0.73% of votes and did not clear the 5% threshold; the party remained legally registered, was not liquidated, and continues to exist in the current period in a marginalised status. This is an example of the fourth trajectory — minimal electoral activity with retention of party status — and simultaneously an indicator that liquidation is not the automatic consequence of low electoral results; a liquidation decision is the outcome of a separate administrative procedure initiated by the Ministry of Justice before the Supreme Court.

Drawing on the source 02-cross-cutting/08-extra-parliamentary-liberals.md, one can identify four life trajectories for non-parliamentary parties: (1) merger with a systemic party — 'Party of Growth' → New People; Babkin/'Party of Business' → LDPR; (2) backing a non-systemic candidate → liquidation — 'Civic Initiative' → Nadezhdin → liquidation; (3) marginalisation — Civil Platform, RPFJ, Green Alternative; (4) minimal electoral activity with retention of status — Yabloko, PARNAS.

In 2024—2025, as a result of three liquidations, two of the four trajectories — (1) and (2) — formally closed with the termination of party structures. The staff of 'Party of Business' and 'Party of Growth' transferred to LDPR and New People respectively — both systemic parliamentary parties. The independent organisational infrastructure for the trajectory 'backing a non-systemic candidate' ceased to exist with the liquidation of 'Civic Initiative'. Trajectories (3) marginalisation and (4) retention of status with minimal activity remain open — that is, the options that do not entail independent electoral mobilisation.
