Mobilisation — in a single day.

On 20 September 2022 the State Duma (Государственная Дума) passed amendments to the Criminal Code on mobilisation in a single plenary day. The final third-reading vote was 389/0/0 (raw XML). All 4 'opposition' factions (CPRF, LDPR, A Just Russia – For Truth, New People) voted 'for' unanimously; not one of their 103 deputies voted 'against' or abstained. Thirty-six hours later, partial mobilisation began (Presidential Decree No. 647 of 21.09.2022). Within 3 months — 300,000+ called up.

A · Law in one day

Chronology of an accelerated passage

Bill No. 160006-8, in its original version of 25.01.2022, was introduced as a technical package on liability for violations during conscription and mobilisation preparedness. As of 19.09.2022, the third reading was not listed on the State Duma's work plan — neither in the draft plenary session agenda nor in the bills published the day before for consideration.

On the morning of 20.09.2022, second-reading amendments were urgently tabled, radically changing the bill's substance: the concepts of 'mobilisation', 'wartime', and 'martial law' were introduced; Articles 207.3, 280.3, and 284.2 were added to the Criminal Code (expanding criminal liability), along with Article 352.1 'Voluntary surrender to the enemy' (up to 10 years) and Article 356.1 'Marauding' (up to 15 years); penalties for desertion and going AWOL were tightened. On the same day, during the afternoon plenary session, the third reading was held: vote_id 119076, result 389/0/0. On the evening of 20.09.2022, the Federation Council convened and approved the bill immediately.

On 21.09.2022 at 09:00, the President signed Decree No. 647 on partial mobilisation — before the law itself was signed. At 17:00, Putin addressed the nation. The law was formally signed by the President only on 24.09.2022 as Federal Law No. 365-FZ. In other words, the legislative infrastructure for mobilisation (including criminal penalties for evading call-up and surrendering to the enemy) was prepared by the 'opposition' factions one day before the first 300,000 men were called up.

B · Faction voting

How all 5 factions voted

On 20.09.2022 in the third reading, 389 deputies voted for the amendments; zero voted against and zero abstained. According to raw XML from api.duma.gov.ru, the factions broke down as follows: UR 286/0/0/38; CPRF 51/0/0/6; LDPR 18/0/0/5; SRZP 21/0/0/7; New People 13/0/0/2. Not one of the 103 voting deputies from the 4 'opposition' factions (CPRF+LDPR+SRZP+NP; total faction membership 123, of whom 20 were absent) voted 'against' or abstained. The 20 absent deputies represent normal background-level non-attendance on a plenary day, not a faction-level demonstration.

Concurrently, Vladislav Davankov (NP) — on that same day — was a co-author of the second-reading amendments on which the third reading was held. Eighteen months later he stood in the 2024 presidential election as 'the peace and negotiation candidate' (3.85%, 3rd place). This is the central paradox of this theme: the author of the Criminal Code provisions on mobilisation was, in 2024, a candidate 'for peace'.

Detailed context on each faction can be found on the party pages: CPRF, LDPR, SRZP, New People.

C · Co-authors of the amendments

Who introduced the substantive provisions

According to the bill card No. 160006-8 and reports by Коммерсантъ, Российская газета, and 72.ru, the substantive content (the concepts of 'wartime', Article 352.1, Article 356.1, and the tightening of liability for desertion) was introduced via second-reading amendments. Among their authors were representatives of all 4 'opposition' factions.

Gennady Zyuganov (КПРФ) (CPRF) — party chairman since 1993. He is not formally listed as an author of the second-reading mobilisation amendments, but by 20.09.2022 he was already a co-author of Federal Law No. 32-FZ of 04.03.2022 (Article 207.3 of the Criminal Code — 'false information about the army', carrying up to 15 years' imprisonment without statute of limitations) — the article that became the basis for criminal prosecution of anti-war statements. The CPRF faction voted unanimously on the mobilisation amendments — 51/0/0/6. More detail on the CPRF party page.

Leonid Slutsky (ЛДПР) (LDPR) — LDPR chairman from 27.05.2022 (elected at the party congress following the death of Vladimir Zhirinovsky on 06.04.2022). Co-author of the second-reading amendments. Before 2022, he chaired the State Duma Committee on International Affairs. He received 3.2% of the vote in the 2024 presidential election. More detail on the LDPR party page.

Oleg Nilov (СРЗП) (A Just Russia – For Truth) — deputy since 2011, deputy leader of the SRZP faction. Co-author of the second-reading mobilisation amendments; simultaneously a co-author of Federal Law No. 32-FZ of 04.03.2022 on 'false information about the army'. His electoral rhetoric of 'defending the social rights of workers' is structurally incompatible with his actual role as co-author of wartime criminal provisions. More detail on the SRZP party page.

Vladislav Davankov (Новые люди) (New People) — Deputy Speaker of the State Duma since 2021, the party's candidate in the 2024 presidential election (3.85%, 3rd place, campaign slogan 'peace and negotiations'). Co-author of the second-reading mobilisation amendments. The central paradox of this theme: the author of the wartime Criminal Code provisions was, in 2024, positioning himself as a 'peace-maker' candidate. More detail on the New People party page.

Andrei Kartapolov (Андрей Картаполов) and Andrei Krasov (Андрей Красов) (United Russia) — chairman and deputy chairman of the State Duma Committee on Defence; the key sponsors of the second-reading amendment package on behalf of the parliamentary majority. Kartapolov is a Colonel-General and former Deputy Minister of Defence; Krasov is a Hero of the Russian Federation.

D · Campaigns 'after'

What happened in the 27 months following the vote

22.09–22.12.2022. In the three months following Presidential Decree No. 647, 300,000+ men were sent to the front (Ministry of Defence estimate). According to indirect estimates by Re:Russia and Meduza, mobilisation affected around 3 million people (those mobilised plus their closest family members). The criminal provisions enacted on 20.09.2022 began being applied to draft evaders and those going AWOL within the first month.

As of December 2024, the joint BBC × Медиазона project has recorded ≥80,000 confirmed names of those killed (only those whose deaths have been verified through public obituaries, gravestone photographs, and regional media; the actual figure is higher). Under Article 352.1 of the Criminal Code (surrender to the enemy) and Articles 337–338 (going AWOL and desertion, tightened by the 20.09.2022 amendments), hundreds of criminal cases had been opened by 2024.

17.03.2024. Presidential election. All three 'opposition' candidates were representatives of factions that had voted 'for' mobilisation: Vladislav Davankov (NP) — 3.85%, 'peace and negotiations'; Nikolai Kharitonov (CPRF) — 4.31%; Leonid Slutsky (LDPR) — 3.2%. Yekaterina Duntsova (Екатерина Дунцова) and Boris Nadezhdin (Борис Надеждин) were refused registration by the CEC (on 23.12.2023 and 08.02.2024 respectively). Davankov himself was a co-author of those same second-reading amendments of 20.09.2022. Structurally, this means that in 2024 only candidates from factions that had voted 'for' the wartime Criminal Code provisions were permitted onto the presidential ballot.
