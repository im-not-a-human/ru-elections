SRZP — an administrative construct
in three waves.

The party most thoroughly documented in history as an 'administrative construct'. On 28 October 2006, Deputy Head of the Presidential Administration Vladislav Surkov co-ordinated the merger of Sergei Mironov's (Сергей Миронов) 'Party of Life', the 'Party of Pensioners', and Alexander Babakov's (Александр Бабаков) 'Rodina' into 'A Just Russia'. On 26 March 2021, First Deputy Head of the Presidential Administration Sergei Kirienko (Сергей Кириенко) merged SR with Zakhar Prilepin's (Захар Прилепин) 'For Truth' structure and Gennady Semigin's (Геннадий Семигин) 'Patriots of Russia' into SRZP (registered by the Ministry of Justice). On 25 October 2025, at the party congress it was renamed back to 'A Just Russia', the institution of co-chairs was abolished, Prilepin was demoted to deputy chairman, and Kirienko publicly addressed the congress with greetings from Putin — the final phase of the curator's legalisation. Anatoly Aksakov (Анатолий Аксаков) (SRZP) — author of Federal Law No. 340-FZ of 24.07.2023 'On the Digital Rouble'; Mironov — co-author of the law banning LGBT 'propaganda' and the law banning gender reassignment; Oleg Nilov (Олег Нилов) — co-author of the mobilisation amendments of 20.09.2022.

A · Origins

Three waves of mergers — Surkov 2006 → Kirienko 2021 → Kirienko at the 2025 congress

SRZP is the most clearly documented case of administrative party construction in Russia's recent history. The party has gone through three waves of mergers and renamings, each publicly accompanied by documented participation from representatives of the Presidential Administration (Surkov → Kirienko). No other parliamentary party has undergone a comparable number of structural transformations: the CPRF and LDPR have retained their formal structures since registration, Yabloko since 1995, and New People is a relatively young project from 2020.

Three waves of mergers and renamings. Arrows indicate mergers and curatorial ties; dashed lines indicate reconstructions via public statements (Prilepin 2021). Click a node for details.

First wave — 28 October 2006: founding of 'A Just Russia'

First wave. On 28 October 2006 in Moscow, a unifying congress was held: the 'Russian Party of Life' of Sergei Mironov (Сергей Миронов) (at the time of the merger — Chairman of the Federation Council since 5.12.2001), the 'Russian Party of Pensioners' of Igor Zotov (Игорь Зотов), and the 'Rodina' party of Alexander Babakov (Александр Бабаков) (following Dmitry Rogozin's (Дмитрий Рогозин) departure to the Presidential Administration) merged into 'A Just Russia' (Большая российская энциклопедия). The documented participation of Vladislav Yuryevich Surkov (Владислав Юрьевич Сурков) — at the time First Deputy Head of the Presidential Administration — is recorded in Forbes, РБК, Kommersant, and in the academic work of Kynev and Golosov: the merger was co-ordinated with the Presidential Administration to create a managed 'left flank' within the architecture of a two-party system. 'Rodina' 2003–2006 was a documented Presidential Administration project under Surkov: as political consultant Marat Gelman (Марат Гельман) admitted to Коммерсантъ, 'Rodina's' purpose was "to take as many votes as possible from the CPRF"; at the election of 7.12.2003 'Rodina' won 9.02% (twice its allocated quota), after which Glazyev (Глазьев) was removed and the party was absorbed into 'A Just Russia' in 2006.

Second wave — 26 March 2021: merger into SRZP

Second wave. January–February 2021 — a manifesto on the merger of three parties: Mironov's 'A Just Russia', Zakhar Prilepin's (Захар Прилепин) 'For Truth' (founded 01.02.2020, registered by the Ministry of Justice 25 March 2020 — the same week as Alexei Nechayev's (Алексей Нечаев) 'New People'), and Gennady Semigin's (Геннадий Семигин) 'Patriots of Russia' (former principal financial donor of the CPRF 1996–2004, expelled by Zyuganov in 2004). On 26 March 2021 the Ministry of Justice registered the amendments to the charter; the new name: 'A Just Russia — For Truth' (SRZP). The co-ordinator of the second wave was First Deputy Head of the Presidential Administration Sergei Vladilенович Kirienko (Сергей Владиленович Кириенко): Prilepin publicly stated that the merger was co-ordinated with the Presidential Administration through Kirienko (documents not publicly published; the parallel registration of 'For Truth' and 'New People' on 25.03.2020 is a structural signal of co-ordinated Kremlin party-building ahead of the 2021 elections). The party shifted towards a military-patriotic profile — Mironov's traditional 'social justice' was supplemented by Prilepin's theme of the Donbas war and Semigin's 'sovereignty'.

Third wave — 25 October 2025: rebrand with a public curator

Third wave. On 25 October 2025, at the congress the party was renamed back to 'A Just Russia' (without the 'For Truth' suffix), the institution of co-chairs was abolished, and Prilepin was demoted to deputy chairman. At the congress Sergei Kirienko addressed it with greetings from President V. V. Putin (Радио Свобода). This is a rare public signal: the First Deputy Head of the Presidential Administration does not ordinarily address the congresses of opposition parties with greetings from the President if a party is not managed by the system. The return to Mironov's socially populist agenda — cementing control of the party under a single leader with the abolition of the alternative-leadership institution; Prilepin as the face of the military-patriotic wing moves into a subordinate position. By 2025, SRZP is the most openly managed party in the system; this is no longer a journalistic hypothesis but a publicly documented fact.

B · Financing

76.2% from the budget in 2024 — the lowest share among parliamentary opposition parties

SRZP has the lowest share of state budget financing among parliamentary opposition parties — 76.2% according to the 2024 report, with combined revenues of ≈839 million roubles (≈639 million from the budget; 17 million — loans from legal entities). For comparison: CPRF — 84.4%, LDPR — 89.4%, New People — ≈92–93%, Yabloko — 0% (the party has received no state funding since 2003). The calculation base is the formula under Art. 33 of Federal Law No. 95-FZ 'On Political Parties': 152 roubles × 4,201,058 votes = ≈638.6 million roubles/year in annual budget payments until the next Duma election. This explains why the party's combined revenues in 2022–2025 fluctuate around 720–840 million roubles: between elections the main source of income is precisely the FZ-95 formula.

The structural legacy of three mergers — private donors

SRZP's lower budget share is not a sign of 'independence' but a structural legacy of three mergers — the party retains a relatively larger network of private donors inherited from the structures of Zotov's 'Party of Pensioners', Babakov's 'Rodina', and the pension-sector structures controlled through V. Hartung (Гартунг) in the Chelyabinsk region. Documented historical donors for 2015–2023 include IC 'RUSS-INVEST' (19.4 million roubles in 2015), PJSC 'Chelyabinsk Forging and Pressing Plant' (CHKPZ) (an asset of deputy V. Hartung — a defence-sector contractor operating under contracts with the Ministry of Defence, Rostec, and Almaz-Antei), as well as OOO 'Investstroy-N' (5 million roubles + 3 million in loans in 2023). At the same time, the party did not field its own candidate in the 2024 presidential election and publicly endorsed Vladimir Putin — meaning there was no separate electoral fund from the party in the 2024 campaign; it is the only parliamentary party in 2024 to have declined to nominate a candidate.

Multi-dimensional financial infrastructure

Structurally, SRZP's financial infrastructure is multi-dimensional: budget receipts (76% — the main channel), donations from investment companies (IC 'RUSS-INVEST'), the defence sector via Hartung's enterprise, and regional commercial capital via Babakov's structures. This distinguishes the party from the 'purely' budget-funded projects of LDPR and New People, which have virtually no corporate donors, but does not make it 'independent' from the state: its key corporate donors either hold state contracts (CHKPZ via zakupki.gov.ru) or operate in state-regulated markets (investment companies on Russia's stock market). The journalistic hypothesis about donations from structures linked to Yuri Kovalchuk (Юрий Ковальчук) (bank 'Rossiya') to the 'For Truth' campaign of 2020 is an open point of verification; no direct documentary evidence exists in open sources.

C · Leaders

Mironov + Prilepin + Aksakov — three functional layers of the party

The SRZP leadership team is organised around three functional layers: socially populist (Mironov, Hartung — criticism of the Central Bank's rate, utility tariffs), military-patriotic (Prilepin, Semigin — Donbas, the special military operation, 'Russian world'), and financial-technocratic (Aksakov — digital rouble, banking regulation). The rebrand of 25.10.2025 strengthened the role of the socially populist layer (Mironov) and downgraded the military-patriotic one (Prilepin), while preserving the technocratic layer.

S. M. Mironov — party chairman

Sergei Mikhailovich Mironov (Сергей Михайлович Миронов) (b. 14.02.1953, Pushkin, Leningrad Oblast) — party chairman of 'A Just Russia' / SRZP at all stages since 2006. Airborne forces (1971–1973), Leningrad Mining Institute (1980, geophysics). In 2000 — deputy head of V. V. Putin's electoral campaign headquarters in St Petersburg. On 5 December 2001 he was elected Chairman of the Federation Council (recommended by the President); he held the post until his recall on 18.05.2011. From 2002 to 2011 — a permanent member of the Security Council of Russia (by virtue of his position as Federation Council Chairman). Since 11 July 2012 — a member of the State Council of Russia (by Presidential Decree). Subject to international sanctions from the EU, United Kingdom, USA, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, and Japan since 2022. Co-author of the law banning LGBT 'propaganda' (No. 217471-8, Federal Law No. 478-FZ of 05.12.2022) and the law banning gender reassignment (No. 376846-8, Federal Law No. 386-FZ of 24.07.2023). Mironov's biography is the classic profile of 'a parliamentary leader with a firm state-institutional trajectory': Airborne forces → Putin's 2000 campaign → Federation Council → Security Council → deputy → State Council.

A. G. Aksakov — a record six consecutive convocations and author of the digital rouble

Anatoly Gennadyevich Aksakov (Анатолий Геннадьевич Аксаков) (b. 28.11.1957) — Duma deputy for six consecutive convocations since 1999 (more than 25 years continuously — a record for a non-party functionary). Since 5 October 2016 — chair of the Duma Committee on Financial Markets (re-elected 12.10.2021). 2002–2009 — member of the National Banking Council of the Central Bank of Russia (by Presidential appointment); since 2006 — chair of the Council of the Association of Russian Banks; member of the RSPP (Russian Union of Industrialists and Entrepreneurs) board. Aksakov is the author of Federal Law No. 340-FZ of 24.07.2023 'On the Digital Rouble' (bills No. 270838-8 and No. 270852-8). The law assigned the Central Bank of Russia the status of operator of the digital-rouble platform; at the Duma vote of 11.07.2023 (third reading) — unanimous 'in favour' across all five factions. Structurally Aksakov is a functional intermediary node: deputy → chair of a key Duma committee → chair of the banking association → RSPP board member. This is a 'big-capital functionary with parliamentary backing', not an opposition deputy — the 'opposition' party SRZP, through him, actively constructs the infrastructure of digital financial control.

Z. Prilepin — symbolic instrument of rebranding

Zakhar Prilepin (Евгений Николаевич Прилепин, b. 07.07.1975, Ilyinka, Ryazan Oblast). OMON commander in Nizhny Novgorod in Chechnya (1996, 1999); 2014 — humanitarian missions to Donbas; 2016–2018 — major in the DPR army, deputy battalion commander of the DPR People's Militia. January 2023 — contract with Rosgvardiya, deputy commander of the special-purpose regiment 'Oplot'. On 6 May 2023 in the village of Pionerskoye, Nizhny Novgorod Oblast — an explosion destroyed Prilepin's Audi Q7: driver Alexander Shubin (a former 'Oplot' fighter) was killed; Prilepin suffered fractures to both legs. Suspect Alexander Permyakov was sentenced to life imprisonment; responsibility was claimed by 'Atesh'. The Order of Courage from the President of Russia — Presidential Decree No. 416 of 06.06.2023 (presented in the Kremlin; the same award was given posthumously to the deceased driver A. Shubin). From the time of the 2021 merger until 25.10.2025 — co-chair of the party; after the congress — deputy chairman (demoted; institution of co-chairs abolished).

O. A. Nilov — co-author of mobilisation and migration restrictions

Oleg Anatolyevich Nilov (Олег Анатольевич Нилов) — Duma deputy since 2011, member of the SRZP faction. Co-author of the mobilisation amendments of 20.09.2022 (No. 160006-8, Federal Law No. 365-FZ of 24.09.2022) together with Slutsky (Слуцкий) of LDPR: the only case of joint authorship between two 'opposition' parties on military legislation. The law introduces the concepts of 'mobilisation' and 'martial law', and stiffens penalties for desertion, voluntary surrender (up to 10 years), and looting (up to 15 years); the Duma vote was unanimous. In 2024–2025 — an active co-author of migration restrictions; SRZP, through him, is the most active faction in initiatives to tighten the migration regime.

A. M. Babakov, V. K. Hartung, D. A. Kuznetsov

Alexander Mikhailovich Babakov (Александр Михайлович Бабаков) (b. 08.02.1963) — former leader of 'Rodina' 2003–2006, who joined 'A Just Russia' after the 2006 merger; deputy party chairman, Duma deputy, and in the 8th convocation — Deputy Speaker of the State Duma. Under sanctions from the USA, EU, Canada, and the United Kingdom. According to iStories investigations, Babakov has documented ties to Ukraine's energy sector (enterprises in the energy industry, former assets); investigations claim that some Ukrainian assets survived past 2014 (an open point of verification). Valery Karlovich Hartung (Валерий Карлович Гартунг) (b. 12.11.1960, Chelyabinsk) — co-owner of PJSC 'Chelyabinsk Forging and Pressing Plant' (CHKPZ, INN 7449006184) — a major defence-sector contractor; Duma deputy across several convocations; in the 8th convocation — chair of the Duma Committee on Competition Protection. Hartung is a direct example of 'a leader's business with state contracts' inside a parliamentary party. Dmitry Anatolyevich Kuznetsov (Дмитрий Анатольевич Кузнецов) — 8th-convocation deputy, known as the sole abstainer at the vote on electronic summonses of 11.04.2023, who publicly stated that he had 'pressed the wrong button', declining any political interpretation of the episode.

D · State ties

Four lines of integration — supreme state bodies, financial regulator, security forces, defence sector

SRZP is the most multi-dimensionally embedded in the state of all five parliamentary parties (after United Russia). The party's leaders occupy four key lines of integration: supreme state bodies (Mironov: Federation Council, Security Council, State Council); parliamentary-regulatory vertical (Aksakov: chair of the Duma Committee on Financial Markets, Association of Russian Banks, RSPP board); the security-forces vertical (Prilepin: contract with Rosgvardiya, Order of Courage); corporate defence sector (Hartung: CHKPZ as defence contractor). This produces a picture of a party in which each of the key public functionaries occupies a position along one of four axes of state integration.

A distributed sanctions profile

A feature unique among parliamentary parties — all leading figures are subject to sanctions. Chairman Mironov, Deputy Speaker Babakov, chair of the Duma Committee on Financial Markets Aksakov, deputies Kabychev (Кабышев), Prilepin, and a number of other faction deputies are simultaneously subject to sanctions from the USA, EU, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, and Japan. This structurally distinguishes SRZP from the CPRF (where the main sanction episodes concern a single figure — Zyuganov) and from LDPR (where the principal sanctioned figure is Lugovoy (Луговой) in the Litvinenko case). SRZP is the only parliamentary opposition party whose sanctions profile is distributed across the entire leadership; this publicly confirms the international identification of the party as a structural partner in state decisions regarding the special military operation, rather than an autonomous political force.

State awards and ranks

State awards and ranks. Prilepin — the Order of Courage (Decree No. 416 of 06.06.2023, presented by the President in the Kremlin) following the assassination attempt of 6 May 2023; the same award was given posthumously to driver A. Shubin. Mironov — numerous state awards over the period 2001–2024; member of the Presidium of the Council under the President of Russia on Interethnic Relations (2012–2016); various patronage and civic positions. Aksakov — departmental awards from the Central Bank and the Ministry of Finance; member of the Council on Financial Market Development under the Central Bank Governor. Babakov — state awards; chair of the State Council Commission on High-Tech Medicine (certain periods). A party with such a density of awards, ranks, and posts in para-governmental structures is structurally incompatible with any hypothesis of an autonomous political force independent from the state.

Joint projects with state corporations and pro-government business

Joint projects with state corporations and pro-government business. Through Aksakov the party co-ordinates interaction with Sberbank, VTB, and Gazprombank (key state banks) — representing interests through the Association of Russian Banks; through Hartung and CHKPZ — contracts with Almaz-Antei, Rostec, and the Ministry of Defence; through Prilepin and Rosgvardiya — a formal deputy-commander contract for the 'Oplot' regiment from January 2023 (the 'Oplot' regiment's financial flows are an open point of verification). Across all four lines of integration, SRZP is a functional participant in the state system; the party's 'opposition' character amounts to socio-economic rhetoric (criticism of the Central Bank's rate, utility tariffs) and does not extend to the political course.

E · Voting

98% 'in favour' on restrictive laws — a faction that co-authored the key provisions

The SRZP faction's combined rate of support for restrictive and military-repressive laws of 2022–2025 is ≈98–99%. Across 18 key votes in the restrictive bloc of 2019–2025, SRZP voted 'in favour' 89% of the time (16 out of 18); the only deviation episodes are the Klishas package of 2019 (part of the faction voted against), partially the sovereign Runet law of 2019 (part of the faction voted against), and the VPN/extremism-search law of 22.07.2025 (the faction abstained together with part of the CPRF). After 2022 — a single isolated deviation episode (VPN-2025); on military-repressive matters the faction's position is unanimous 'in favour' with direct rhetoric from Mironov: "the Duma faction unanimously supported the special military operation".

Not merely 'in favour' — co-authorship of the key provisions

The structural peculiarity of SRZP is that the faction does not merely vote for restrictive laws but co-authors the key provisions. Aksakov — author of Federal Law No. 340-FZ of 24.07.2023 'On the Digital Rouble' (bills No. 270838-8 and No. 270852-8). The law assigned the Central Bank the status of digital-rouble platform operator; the vote of 11.07.2023 (third reading) — unanimous 'in favour'. Aksakov is the public face of lobbying for CBDC implementation in Russia; the 'opposition' party, through him, actively constructs the infrastructure of digital financial control — that is, an instrument that any party with a genuine liberal-social programme defending civil liberties would structurally be expected to oppose.

Mironov and Nilov — authorial roles in the military and ideological agenda

Mironov — co-author of the law banning 'propaganda of non-traditional sexual relations' (No. 217471-8, Federal Law No. 478-FZ of 05.12.2022) and the law banning gender reassignment (No. 376846-8, Federal Law No. 386-FZ of 24.07.2023). Among the co-authors of the first law are the leaders of all five factions; the third-reading vote of 24.11.2022 was unanimous. This transforms Mironov from a 'yes-voter' into an authorial figure on the cultural-conservative agenda. Oleg Nilov — co-author of the mobilisation amendments (No. 160006-8, Federal Law No. 365-FZ of 24.09.2022) together with Slutsky of LDPR; the law was signed on the day of the announcement of partial mobilisation (21.09.2022). Mironov and Nilov are also co-authors of the migration restrictions of 2024–2025; SRZP is the most active faction on this front.

Anomalies — conscription age raised to 30 and the split vote on VPN

Anomaly of 25 July 2023: Federal Law No. 439-FZ raising the conscription age to 30. SRZP was the only faction to abstain as a bloc (~23 deputies abstained). In Duma practice this does not mean public disagreement but tactical evasion of formal accountability: with no 'against' votes the law passes on the strength of United Russia's majority, but the faction insulates itself from direct association with an unpopular measure expanding the conscription pool. This is not opposition — it is neutralising political accountability while maintaining the overall course. The 'pressed the wrong button' episode with Dmitry Kuznetsov (11.04.2023, the sole abstainer on the electronic-summonses vote, with his public refusal to give it any political interpretation) further illustrates the point: even a single technical anomaly in the faction's voting is accompanied by a refusal to acknowledge it politically. On Federal Law No. 281-FZ on VPN/extremism search (No. 755710-8) of 22 July 2025, SRZP voted in a mixed fashion — partly 'in favour', partly 'against', with the majority abstaining; this is the only case of a split faction vote during the crisis period 2022–2025.

F · Managed opposition

The most thoroughly documented case of administrative party construction

SRZP is the most thoroughly documented example of administrative party construction. Unlike other parties, where the hypothesis of 'co-ordination with the Presidential Administration' remains a journalistic reconstruction, in the case of SRZP — three waves of mergers and renamings were publicly accompanied by signals of Presidential Administration involvement: Surkov in 2006 (documented), Kirienko in 2021 (journalistic reconstruction via Prilepin's own statements), and Kirienko in 2025 (documented through his address to the congress with greetings from Putin). This structurally makes SRZP the most open-book case of a 'managed party' in the system.

Institutional-connections graph: Presidential Administration → Surkov 2006 + Kirienko 2021/2025 → SRZP leaders. Parallel channel to New People via 25.03.2020 (same week of registration as 'For Truth'). All links are publicly documented or reconstructed via public statements.

The technology of 'managing a party through mergers'

The technology of 'managing a party through mergers'. In the period 2006–2025, SRZP passes through four structural transformations: 2006 (merger of three parties into SR); 2007 (absorption of the Social Democratic Party of Russia); 2021 (merger with 'For Truth' and 'Patriots of Russia' into SRZP); 2025 (rebrand back to 'A Just Russia' and Prilepin's demotion). In 2006 the merger occupies the 'social justice' niche, absorbing part of the pensioners' electorate and the national-patriotic electorate of 'Rodina'; in 2021 — expansion into the military-patriotic field ahead of the 2021 elections; in 2025 — a return to Mironov's socially populist agenda while retaining the military-patriotic branding. The CPRF, LDPR, and Yabloko have retained their formal structures since founding/re-registration; SRZP is the only parliamentary party to have undergone this many transformations.

The 2021 merger as a dual channel — in parallel with New People

The 2021 merger as a dual channel — in parallel with New People. Prilepin's 'For Truth' was founded on 01.02.2020 and registered by the Ministry of Justice on 25 March 2020 — the same week as Alexei Nechayev's 'New People'. This parallel registration of two new parties is a structural signal of co-ordinated Kremlin party-building ahead of the 2021 elections: 'For Truth' expands the military-patriotic electorate niche through its merger with SR; 'New People' opens the 'technocratic liberalism' niche through the first Duma mandate for a non-parliamentary party since 2003. Both projects clear the 5% electoral threshold at the 2021 elections; both enter the 8th-convocation Duma. By 2025 both projects are institutionally stabilised: New People absorbs Titov's (Титов) Party of Growth in April 2024; SRZP undergoes the rebrand of 25.10.2025 with a direct public demonstration of its curator in the person of Kirienko.

Electoral monolithism and the spoiler

Electorally the party has no public dissidents; the faction's voting is unanimous on all key restrictive laws. The external spoiler party is the 'Party of Pensioners for Justice' (registered by the Ministry of Justice in 2012; at the 2021 Duma election — 2.45%), ideologically close to the 'social justice' niche with an emphasis on pensioners. At the 2024 presidential election, SRZP was the only parliamentary party not to nominate its own candidate; the party publicly endorsed V. V. Putin. This is a structural signal of the party's 'functional self-abnegation': it does not use the available option of fielding a candidate — which in a normal party system would weaken its electoral position. In terms of the research's working thesis, SRZP is the fullest embodiment of the concept of 'managed opposition'.

G · Crises and war

Behaviour in the acute moments of 2022–2026 — consistent, active support for the course

At the crisis points of 2022–2026, SRZP demonstrates consistent and actively productive support for the state course. On the war — unanimous 'in favour' and Mironov's public rhetoric of "unanimous support for the special military operation"; on mobilisation — Nilov as co-author; on the LGBT ban and the ban on gender reassignment — Mironov as co-author; on the digital rouble — Aksakov as author; on migration — Mironov and Nilov as co-authors; on the 2024 presidential election — declining to nominate a candidate and endorsing Putin. 'Genuine opposition' is limited exclusively to the socio-economic domain — voting 'against' the 2024–2026 budget (17.11.2023, unanimously with the CPRF) and abstaining on the 2025–2027 budget (21.11.2024, together with the CPRF). On freedoms — zero opposition; on the war — unanimous 'in favour'.

The assassination attempt on Prilepin of 6 May 2023 did not alter the party's line

The assassination attempt on Prilepin of 6 May 2023 did not alter the party's line. Following the explosion of the Audi Q7 in the village of Pionerskoye, Nizhny Novgorod Oblast, the death of driver A. Shubin, and Prilepin's severe injuries, the party issued no programmatic revisions to its agenda; after his recovery Prilepin retained his public role as SRZP co-chair until the rebrand of 25.10.2025. This structurally shows that the party is not personalised around Prilepin (as LDPR was personalised around Zhirinovsky (Жириновский) before 2022) but functions as a structural construct without dependence on any individual figure. The Order of Courage for Prilepin (Decree No. 416 of 06.06.2023) cemented the integration of the party's military-patriotic wing into the state system.

The rebrand of 25 October 2025 — public demonstration of Presidential Administration management

The rebrand of 25 October 2025 — public demonstration of Presidential Administration management. At the congress the institution of co-chairs was abolished, Prilepin was demoted to deputy chairman, and the party reverted to 'A Just Russia'. Sergei Kirienko (First Deputy Head of the Presidential Administration) addressed the congress with greetings from the President — a rare public signal leaving no room for interpreting the party as an autonomous political force. The biographical profiles of the party's leaders (Mironov, Aksakov, Prilepin, Hartung, Babakov), the behavioural pattern of the faction 2022–2026 (98–99% 'in favour'), and the public demonstration of its curator in the person of Kirienko at the congress are incompatible with any hypothesis that SRZP is a political force autonomous from the state.

H · Foreign ties

No foreign financing documented; sanctions profile distributed across leadership

Under Art. 30 of Federal Law No. 95-FZ 'On Political Parties', foreign donations to Russian parties are explicitly prohibited. In the public grant databases of the National Endowment for Democracy (NED), Open Society Foundations, Heinrich Böll Stiftung, Friedrich Ebert Stiftung, Friedrich Naumann Stiftung, MacArthur, Ford, and Carnegie there are no records of SRZP financing. For a party positioning itself as social-patriotic with active support for the special military operation and the annexation of four regions, the absence of foreign financing is structurally expected.

Distributed sanctions profile and Babakov's ties to Ukrainian energy

A particular story is the distributed sanctions profile of the party's leadership. Chairman Mironov, Deputy Speaker Babakov, chair of the Duma Committee on Financial Markets Aksakov, deputies Kabychev and Prilepin, and a number of other faction deputies are simultaneously subject to sanctions from the USA, EU, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, and Japan. These are foreign sanctions, not foreign financing — the topic technically falls outside this section but is noted here for completeness: SRZP is the only parliamentary opposition party whose sanctions profile is distributed across the entire leadership rather than concentrated in a single figure (as Lugovoy in LDPR in the Litvinenko case). According to iStories investigations, Babakov has documented ties to Ukraine's energy sector (an open point of verification). A full review of the foreign ties of all Russian parties and sanctions episodes is in the file 04-foreign-ties.md.
