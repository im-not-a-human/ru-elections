A · Origins

1993 — re-establishment of the party; Zyuganov as Chairman of the Central Committee since 22.01.1995

The modern Communist Party of the Russian Federation (Коммунистическая партия Российской Федерации) was re-established at the 2nd 'Restoration' Congress on 13–14 February 1993 in the Moscow Oblast. The legal basis was a ruling of the Constitutional Court of 30 November 1992. The Chairman of the Central Committee since 22 January 1995 has been Gennady Andreyevich Zyuganov; more than 30 years of continuous leadership — a record for any post-Soviet Russian party. Per Ministry of Justice data, KPRF was re-registered and entered into the Unified State Register of Legal Entities on 9 September 2002.

The restoration congress and legal basis

The modern Communist Party of the Russian Federation was re-established at the 2nd 'Restoration' Congress on 13–14 February 1993 in the Moscow Oblast. The legal basis was a ruling of the Constitutional Court of 30 November 1992, which found partly unconstitutional the decrees of Boris Yeltsin "On the activities of the CPSU and the Communist Party of the RSFSR" (23 August 1991) and "On the property of the CPSU and the Communist Party of the RSFSR" (25 August 1991) — specifically the dissolution of primary organisations. This unblocked the possibility of the restoration congress.

The congress was formally designated the 'second' due to legal continuity with the Communist Party of the RSFSR, established in June 1990 and suspended by Yeltsin's decree of 23 August 1991. Under the requirements of Federal Law No. 95-FZ "On Political Parties" of 11 July 2001, KPRF was re-registered and entered into the Unified State Register of Legal Entities on 9 September 2002 — one of the oldest currently active Russian parties by date of registration.

Zyuganov — record of continuous leadership

Gennady Andreyevich Zyuganov was elected Chairman of the Central Executive Committee; at the 3rd Congress in January 1995, following an amendment to the party rules, the post was redesignated Chairman of the KPRF Central Committee. The date 22 January 1995 is taken as the formal starting date of Zyuganov's uninterrupted tenure — today more than thirty years, a record of party leadership in post-Soviet Russia. Forbes (2013) described the party as "a complex limited company in which Zyuganov holds the controlling stake": behind the formal democratic procedure of congresses, real control over the apparatus and finances is concentrated in the hands of the CC Chairman and a narrow group of historical allies (I. Melnikov, Yu. Afonin, and previously V. Kuptsov and G. Semigin until his expulsion in 2004).

Zyuganov's status as an irremovable leader was legally cemented in 2014 by amendments to the party rules permitting re-election of the CC Chairman an unlimited number of times; before that, the rules provided for a limit of two consecutive terms. In terms of succession, KPRF positions itself as the ideological heir of the CPSU, but not its legal successor in a property sense — after 1991–1992 the CPSU's property base was not transferred to the re-established party.

The ideological 'left-conservative synthesis'

Ideologically, the party programme (last revised in 2008) combines classic Soviet rhetoric with a conservative-patriotic component, which is reflected in the voting record of 2022–2025: KPRF votes 'yes' on culturally conservative restrictions while simultaneously criticising the government's socio-economic policy. This 'left-conservative synthesis' distinguishes KPRF from classic post-war European communist and left-wing parties and aligns it more closely with national-patriotic movements.

The generational composition of the party remains predominantly ageing: the average age of deputies in the KPRF faction in the 8th State Duma is around 60; the average age of the top five candidates on KPRF's federal list in the 2021 Duma elections was 67 (Zyuganov b. 1944, Afonin b. 1977, Melnikov b. 1950, Kharitonov b. 1948, Novikov b. 1969). This creates a structural risk to the party's stability in the medium term and explains the growing attention to the problem of a 'post-Zyuganov' transition.

Electoral trajectory 1995–2024

The electoral inheritance of the 1990s remains the material basis for the party's self-positioning. At the 1995 Duma elections KPRF received 22.3% and became the largest faction; in 1999 — 24.29%; at the 1996 presidential election Zyuganov reached the run-off against Boris Yeltsin and obtained 40.31%. The best result in the period 2003–2025 was 18.93% (10.66 million votes) at the Duma elections on 17–19 September 2021. That result was the highest in 13 years (since the 2008 Duma elections) and was interpreted by party leadership as a 'second wind' against the backdrop of the 2020 constitutional reform and social discontent over the 2018 pension reform. However, the subsequent presidential campaign of Nikolai Kharitonov (4.31% in 2024) showed the reverse — KPRF's worst result in 25 years.

B · Financing

84–94% state-budget financing; 1.62 bn ₽/year under Art. 33 of ФЗ-95

In 2022–2024 84–94% of party revenues came from the state budget. According to Коммерсантъ and the Golos report, the budget share reached a record 94% in 2023 and 84.4% in 2024. The calculation base — the rate under Art. 33 of ФЗ-95: 152 ₽ × 10,660,669 votes = 1.62 bn ₽/year in annual budget transfers until the next Duma elections.

The 152 ₽/vote rate — the calculation base

Under the current version of Federal Law No. 95-FZ "On Political Parties" (Article 33, as amended in 2017), state financing of political parties amounts to 152 roubles for each vote cast for the party at the Duma elections. Financing is paid annually for five years until the next Duma elections. At the 2021 Duma elections on 17–19 September, 10,660,669 voters cast their ballot for KPRF (18.93% turnout-adjusted). The calculated annual budget flow: 152 ₽ × 10,660,669 = ≈1.62 bn ₽/year. This explains why the party's total revenues in the inter-election years (2022, 2023) fluctuate around 1.7–1.9 bn ₽ with a budget share of 84–94% — the main source of revenue between elections is precisely the transfers from the federal budget under the ФЗ-95 rate.

Revenue breakdown 2021–2025

Summary table of party revenues by year:

Structural paradox — anti-capitalist rhetoric on public money

Structural paradox: a party that ideologically declares an anti-capitalist and anti-bourgeois course is materially sustained 84–94% by the state budget of the very capitalist system it formally opposes. The party's financial independence from its own electorate (the share of donations from private individuals is low) renders it directly dependent on maintaining a sufficient electoral result to preserve its status as a recipient of state-budget financing. This creates an upper ceiling on permissible public opposition: any fall below the 5% threshold automatically cuts the party off from ≈1.6 bn ₽/year.

Top donors 2020–2023

The largest corporate donors to KPRF for 2020–2023 (per Golos reconstruction): the National Project XXI Century Foundation — ≈40 m ₽ (an NGO effectively affiliated with the party); LLC "UK Zarubezhenergoproekt" (Ivanovo) — 40 m ₽ in donations + 21 m ₽ in interest-free loans, whose general director until 2017 was Sergei Natsievsky, a deputy of the Ivanovo Regional Duma for KPRF; the all-Russian organisation "Children of the War" — ≈24.5 m ₽ (chair — KPRF deputy Nikolai Arefyev); and until 2018 — the Lenin State Farm (Sovkhoz im. Lenina) of Pavel Grudinin (donations ceased following Grudinin's expulsion). The party has no large independent corporate donors outside the deputy-party circuit.

A KPRF deputy as beneficiary of a company that donates to the party is a structurally typical pattern across all parliamentary factions; for a party that ideologically declares opposition to "oligarchic capitalism", the combination of deputy-critic and shareholder of a donor company creates its own contradiction.

The 2024 presidential campaign — the paradox of 'worst result, best profit'

The 2024 presidential campaign proved the most financially efficient at the worst electoral result. Candidate Nikolai Kharitonov received 4.31% (3,768,470 votes) — KPRF's worst result in a presidential election in 25 years. Simultaneously, according to calculations by Forbes (March 2024), the party's net profit from the campaign was ≈407 m ₽ — more than LDPR (≈220 m) and New People (≈150 m). The 'worst result — most profitable campaign' paradox is explained by cost minimisation: against a permissible campaign-fund ceiling of 400 m ₽, the campaign actually spent less than 200 m ₽, and the remainder after CEC reimbursement formed the party's 'profit'. Low electoral ambition combined with high financial efficiency — a characteristic case of 'opposition in form, accountancy in substance'.

  • KPRF — 1.8 bn ₽; budget share 84.4% (152 ₽ × 10.66 m = 1.62 bn ₽).
  • LDPR — 767 m ₽; budget share 89.4% (152 ₽ × 4.25 m = 646 m ₽).
  • SRZP — 726 m ₽; budget share 76.2% (152 ₽ × 4.20 m = 638 m ₽).
  • New People — 719 m ₽; budget share ≈93% (152 ₽ × 2.99 m = 455 m ₽).
  • Yabloko — 189 m ₽; budget share 0% (below the 3% threshold since 2016).

KPRF is the largest systemic opposition war-chest in the country, financially 2.4 times larger than LDPR. The budget share of 84–94% renders the party financially entirely dependent on the state budget.

C · Leaders

Zyuganov 30+ years in post; grandchildren in the Moscow City Duma and the Rostov Oblast Government

KPRF's leadership is formed by a stable core of functionaries born between 1944 and 1955 (Zyuganov, Melnikov, Kharitonov, Ostanina) with a generational gap through to the leader's young grandsons (Leonid Zyuganov b. 1988, Mikhail Zyuganov). Zyuganov has been a member of Russia's State Council since 2012, a recipient of six major state orders, and Hero of Labour of Russia (26.06.2024). Family ties through grandsons holding positions in the Moscow City Duma (Leonid) and in the executive branch of Rostov Oblast (Mikhail) — a pattern unique among parliamentary 'opposition' parties.

Zyuganov — biography and state posts

Gennady Andreyevich Zyuganov (Геннадий Андреевич Зюганов) (b. 26.06.1944) — Chairman of the KPRF Central Committee since 22 January 1995, member of the State Duma for convocations I–VIII continuously since 1993. Before the Soviet collapse — Deputy Head of the Ideology Department of the CPSU Central Committee. Profile on duma.gov.ru. Member of the State Council of Russia since 5 June 2012. Recipient of six major state orders ("For Services to the Fatherland" grades IV, III, II — 1999, 2004, 2014; Alexander Nevsky — 2019; Friendship; Honour). On 26 June 2024, on his 80th birthday, he was awarded the title Hero of Labour of the Russian Federation (Presidential Decree No. 544 of 26.06.2024) — the highest distinction for labour achievement; fellow recipients include Igor Sechin, Ramzan Kadyrov, and Sergei Lavrov.

Afonin, Melnikov — the inner circle

Yuri Vyacheslavovich Afonin (b. 22.04.1977, Tula) — First Deputy Chairman of the KPRF Central Committee since 2017, State Duma deputy since 2004. The party's main public spokesperson on military-patriotic affairs. At the plenary session of 3 October 2022 on ratification of the treaties incorporating the DNR, LNR, Zaporizhzhia, and Kherson oblasts into the Russian Federation, he formulated the party's position: "Confidently, unanimously, and with great hope and joy, we shall adopt this decision." Ivan Ivanovich Melnikov (b. 07.08.1950) — Professor of Mathematical Cybernetics at Moscow State University, State Duma deputy since 1995, First Deputy Speaker of the State Duma across several convocations since 2011, Zyuganov's ally since 1995.

Kalashnikov, Sinelshchikov, Novikov, Kurinny

Leonid Igoryevich Kalashnikov — Chair of the State Duma Committee on CIS Affairs, Eurasian Integration, and Relations with Compatriots; co-author of the appeal for recognition of the DNR/LNR (introduced jointly with Zyuganov and adopted on 15.02.2022). Yuri Petrovich Sinelshchikov (Юрий Петрович Синельщиков) — Deputy Chair of the State Duma Committee on State Construction; from the floor on 4 March 2022 he defended ФЗ-32 on false information about the army: "the law will not permit discrediting information about the Russian Armed Forces; we expect it will not affect journalists." Dmitry Georgiyevich Novikov — Deputy Chairman of the Central Committee; a functionary of the 'ideological' wing. Alexei Vladimirovich Kurinny — one of the few Communists who voted on the merits against the 'sovereign Runet' law of 2019 and ФЗ-281 on VPN/extremism-search of 22.07.2025.

Ostanina — the public face of 'critique from the right'

Nina Alexandrovna Ostanina (Нина Александровна Останина) (b. 26.12.1955) — Chair of the State Duma Committee for the Protection of the Family, Maternity, Fatherhood, and Childhood; co-author of the law banning LGBT "propaganda" (ФЗ № 478-ФЗ of 05.12.2022) and the laws banning "childfree propaganda" (ФЗ № 411-ФЗ + 401-ФЗ of 23.11.2024); in February 2024 she promoted criminal liability for repeat LGBT "propaganda". The public face of KPRF as 'critique from the right' — the party does not oppose restrictions but frequently demands their tightening.

Shargunov and Matveyev — the faction's 'release valves'

Sergei Alexandrovich Shargunov (Сергей Александрович Шаргунов) (b. 12.05.1980) — writer, journalist, editor-in-chief of the journal Svobodnaya Pressa. State Duma deputy since 2016, elected on the KPRF list (formally not a party member). Son of the Orthodox priest Alexander Shargunov, a representative of the conservative-patriotic wing of the Russian Orthodox Church. One of only three deputies in the entire State Duma who voted 'against' at the first reading of the law on confiscation of assets for false information on 24 January 2024 (Law No. 11-FZ of 14.02.2024, Bill No. 521381-8). The other two who voted 'against' were Sardana Avksentyeva and Ksenia Goryacheva of the New People faction. Within KPRF he functions as a 'release valve' for the culturally-patriotic intelligentsia.

Mikhail Nikolayevich Matveyev (b. 30.05.1968) — State Duma deputy for KPRF since 2021 (Samara single-member constituency). Candidate of Historical Sciences. The only consistent 'rebel' within the faction: the only deputy in the entire State Duma who voted 'against' the law on Russia's non-execution of ECHR rulings (June 2022); the only 'against' on ФЗ-63 extending 'false information' provisions to state bodies abroad (22.03.2022); did not vote on digital control of migrants on 20.05.2025. Matveyev's continued membership of the faction at such a level of divergence is a structural argument for understanding the faction as a container of diversified positions within a disciplined overall line.

Kharitonov — 2024 candidate; the Zyuganov family dynasty

Nikolai Mikhailovich Kharitonov (b. 30.10.1948) — State Duma deputy since 1993; presidential candidate for KPRF in 2004 (13.69%, 2nd place) and in 2024 (4.31%, 2nd place). The 2024 presidential campaign — the weakest in the party's 25-year existence. Family dimension: Zyuganov's grandsons — Leonid Zyuganov (b. 27.12.1988) — deputy of the Moscow City Duma (convocations V, VI, VII) since 2009, head of the KPRF faction in the Moscow City Duma since 2024, candidate for Mayor of Moscow 2023 (8.11%); Mikhail Zyuganov — since August 2025, Deputy Minister of Economic Development of Rostov Oblast. Family integration into the executive branch of a federal subject has no parallel among other parliamentary 'opposition' parties.

Declarations and foreign real estate

According to the faction leadership's declarations: Zyuganov — income of 5.4 m ₽ for 2023, assets in the standard middle-class range; Afonin — income of around 7 m ₽; Kurinny — 6.2 m ₽. Most faction deputies declare annual income in the 5–8 m ₽ range, which corresponds to Duma average figures. No foreign citizenship, residence permits, or overseas real estate attributable to Zyuganov, Afonin, Melnikov, Kurinny, Ostanina, or Kharitonov has been documented in open sources. KPRF is the only parliamentary faction whose leaders ideologically reject residence abroad.

D · State ties

State Council since 2012, 6 orders + Hero of Labour 2024, grandsons in the Moscow City Duma and the Rostov Oblast Government

KPRF is structurally integrated into the state elite through three channels: institutional (Zyuganov as a member of Russia's State Council since 2012, recipient of six major orders, and Hero of Labour of Russia); familial (the leader's grandsons in posts within the Moscow City Duma and the executive branch of Rostov Oblast); financial, mediated (the largest corporate donors — a company whose general director is a regional Duma deputy for KPRF).

Zyuganov — member of the State Council since 5 June 2012

Zyuganov has been a member of Russia's State Council since 5 June 2012. He was included in the updated membership by Presidential Decree as leader of a parliamentary party. Following the 2020 constitutional reform the State Council's status was elevated to that of a constitutional body (Art. 83 of the Constitution as amended); the body participates in the discussion of key domestic and foreign policy questions. The leader of a party that formally declares itself to be in opposition is an official member of a constitutional consultative body attached to the President.

The chain of state awards

Highest state honours. A chain of six major orders spanning 25 years: "For Services to the Fatherland" grade IV (Boris Yeltsin, 1999, for his 55th birthday), grade III (Vladimir Putin, 2004, for his 60th birthday), grade II (Vladimir Putin, 2014, for his 70th birthday); Alexander Nevsky (2019, for his 75th birthday); Friendship; Honour. The culmination — the title Hero of Labour of the Russian Federation (Decree No. 544 of 26 June 2024), for his 80th birthday. The highest degree of recognition was conferred eighteen months before the 2026 Duma elections, which amplifies its significance as a signal to the elite and to voters of the President's trust in the KPRF leader as a reliable political partner.

The regular jubilee escalation of awards (from an order grade IV to grade II, then to Alexander Nevsky, and on his 80th birthday — the highest title of Hero of Labour) formally conforms to state practice for rewarding long-serving party leaders. In substance, this is the public institutional formalisation of Zyuganov's status as an insider at the highest level of the state elite.

Family integration into the executive branch

Family integration into the executive branch. Mikhail Zyuganov, grandson of G. A. Zyuganov, has held the post of Deputy Minister of Economic Development of Rostov Oblast since August 2025 (population ≈4 million, GRP among the top ten Russian regions, a well-developed agricultural and defence-industry base). The appointment was possible only with the institutional agreement of Governor Yu. Slyusar (re-elected in September 2025) and indirectly with federal authority. Leonid Zyuganov is a deputy of the Moscow City Duma across three convocations since 2009; since 2024 — leader of the KPRF faction in the Moscow City Duma. This is a pattern unique among parliamentary 'opposition' parties: no comparable appointments of the families of other parliamentary faction leaders (LDPR, SRZP, New People) at the level of deputy minister of a federal subject have been documented in open sources.

Financial integration through donor legal entities

Financial integration through donor legal entities. The party's largest corporate donors are: a company whose former general director is a regional Duma deputy for KPRF (LLC "UK Zarubezhenergoproekt", S. Natsievsky — 61 m ₽ over 2020–2023); and NGOs effectively affiliated with the party itself ("Children of the War", National Project XXI Century Foundation). Until 2018, the Lenin State Farm (Sovkhoz im. Lenina) of Pavel Grudinin was a consistent party donor while simultaneously receiving state agricultural subsidies from the Ministry of Agriculture. No directly documented state contracts with companies connected to KPRF leaders have been found in open sources — unlike the leaders of New People, SRZP, and LDPR. This is a structurally unique characteristic: KPRF leaders do not hold significant extra-parliamentary business interests with state contracts; integration into the state elite is provided by the institutional channel (State Council, honours) and the family channel (grandsons in the Moscow City Duma and Rostov Oblast), rather than through corporate structures.

Comparative context — parliamentary parties

KPRF — alongside SRZP — is the most institutionally integrated 'opposition' party in the highest echelons of state power, across all parameters combined:

  • KPRF: Zyuganov — member of the State Council since 2012; Hero of Labour 2024; 6 major orders + Hero of Labour; grandsons in the Rostov Oblast Government and the Moscow City Duma.
  • LDPR: Slutsky — ambassador; Chair of the State Duma Committee on International Affairs. No titles at the level of Hero of Labour. Igor Lebedev (Zhirinovsky's son) — changed his name, left Russia, 2021.
  • SRZP: Mironov — member of the State Council; Security Council 2002–2011. Order "For Services to the Fatherland".
  • New People: Nechayev — Order of Friendship (2023); Putin's authorised representative 2018.
  • Yabloko: none.

The party's leaders hold no large business capital (unlike Faberlic's Nechayev or CHKPZ's Gartung), but compensate for this with family integration into the executive branch — a structurally deeper model than the 'leader's business + state contracts' pattern characteristic of other parliamentary factions.

E · Voting

≈94–96% support for restrictions 2022–2025; functional division of opposition

The aggregate share of the KPRF faction's support for restrictive and military-repressive laws in 2022–2025 is ≈94–96%. The central pattern is a functional division: the socio-economic plane — genuine opposition ('against' on the 2018 pension reform, the 2020 constitutional amendments, the 2024 and 2025 budgets, the electoral reform of March 2022, the Klishas package of 2019, the 'sovereign Runet' law of 2019, ФЗ-281 on VPN of 2025); the military-repressive and cultural-ideological plane — unanimous 'yes' (ФЗ-32 on false information about the army 04.03.2022, ratification of DNR/LNR 22.02.2022, mobilisation 20.09.2022, annexation 03.10.2022, LGBT bans 24.11.2022, ban on gender reassignment 14.07.2023, confiscation of assets for false information 31.01.2024).

Methodology and sources

The key behavioural test — ФЗ-32 of 04.03.2022 on 'false information about the army'

The key behavioural test — 4 March 2022, ФЗ No. 32-FZ on false information about the army. The law introduces Article 207.3 of the Criminal Code (up to 15 years for "knowingly false information" about the Russian Armed Forces) and Article 280.3 ('"discrediting" the Russian Armed Forces' — a charge prosecuting ordinary anti-war social-media posts). Third-reading vote: 401 for, 0 against, 0 abstained. The KPRF faction voted in favour en bloc; Yuri Sinelshchikov defended the bill from the floor. A party with an electorate of 10.66 million voters, at a moment of crisis after the start of the war, voted unanimously to criminalise anti-war speech for up to 15 years. This is the second most structurally significant test (after the 2020 Constitution) and the sharpest — it showed that the military-foreign-policy agenda is placed beyond the zone of opposition.

Zyuganov personally — co-author of three restrictive laws

Zyuganov personally is co-author of three restrictive laws, which transforms him from a voter in favour to an author: No. 217471-8 banning LGBT "propaganda" for all ages (ФЗ No. 478-FZ of 05.12.2022); No. 376846-8 banning gender reassignment (ФЗ No. 386-FZ of 24.07.2023); No. 521381-8 on confiscation of assets for false information and the revocation of honorary titles (ФЗ No. 11-FZ of 14.02.2024). Simultaneously, Nina Ostanina is co-author of the 'childfree' laws and the ideologue of tightening LGBT norms. This structurally contradicts the classic left-wing agenda (protection of minorities, secular state) and aligns the party more closely with national-patriotic movements.

The ФЗ-281 case of 22.07.2025 — the only faction 'against' on digital affairs since 2022

The case of 22 July 2025 (ФЗ No. 281-FZ on VPN/extremism-search) — the only instance after ФЗ No. 255-FZ of 2022 of genuine faction-level opposition by KPRF on the digital agenda. The search-related amendment was introduced at the second reading into a bill originally dealing with freight forwarding. Third reading — 306 for, 67 against, 22 abstained; the KPRF faction voted 'against' as a bloc (РБК, 22.07.2025). The content of the objections was not liberal but procedural: the party pointed to violations of the Constitution and the inability of investigators to work effectively on extremist material.

The socio-economic axis — genuine opposition

The socio-economic axis continues to operate: the 2024 budget (No. 448554-8) — faction voted 'against'; the 2025 budget — faction abstained. Zyuganov on the 2025 budget: "the idiotic CBR rate is strangling production" — not ideological criticism but a concrete monetary critique. The axis of real political conflict in the 8th Duma is not liberties, but social policy: KPRF + SRZP vote consolidatedly against the budget and tax policy; UR + LDPR + New People — in favour. On all civil and digital freedoms — the inverse configuration: UR + KPRF + LDPR + SRZP consolidatedly in favour; New People — the only faction with episodic dissent.

Progressive income tax — an example of 'opposition pressure over twenty years'

ФЗ No. 372-FZ of 23.11.2020 — a 15% rate on income above 5 m ₽/year; ФЗ No. 176-FZ of 12.07.2024 — a five-bracket scale of 13–22%, corporate tax from 20→25%. The third reading was supported by all five factions; KPRF demanded a rate of up to 30% for super-high incomes. KPRF had demanded progressive income tax for decades; in 2020 and 2024 the demand was partly realised. This is a rare example of successful 'opposition pressure over twenty years' — but its implementation was delivered by United Russia by Presidential decision, not as a result of a political victory by KPRF.

Final verdict for section E

The behaviour of the KPRF faction in the 7th–8th State Duma divides into two functional circuits: socio-economic opposition (genuine 'against' on the 2018 pension reform, the 2020 constitutional amendments, the 2024 and 2025 budgets, the March 2022 electoral reform, progressive income tax); military-repressive and cultural-ideological support (false information 04.03.2022, DNR/LNR ratification, mobilisation, annexation 03.10.2022, LGBT bans 24.11.2022 — Zyuganov co-author, gender-reassignment ban 14.07.2023 — Zyuganov co-author, digital rouble, foreign-agent laws post-ФЗ-255, childfree — Ostanina co-author, migration packages post-Crocus). Aggregate support rate — ≈94–96%. The key behavioural test — the false-information law of 04.03.2022 — the faction passed as part of a unified parliamentary bloc. The key socio-economic test — the 2024–2025 budgets — the faction passed as genuine opposition. This division defines the institutional nature of KPRF as 'socialist critique of the economy alongside patriotic support for the war and cultural conservatism'.

F · Managed opposition

External spoiler 'Communists of Russia' + 6 cases of neutralising internal opponents

KPRF operates with two instruments of 'managed opposition': external — the spoiler party 'Communists of Russia' (Suraykin until 2022, Malinkovich from 2022) with 'name doppelgangers' in single-member constituencies; internal — the systematic neutralisation of figures capable of independent electoral leadership (Seleznyov, Semigin, Grudinin, Rashkin, Levchenko, Bondarenko). As an organisation, KPRF passively cooperates with the neutralisation mechanisms, limiting itself to rhetorical objections.

Graph of managed opposition: KPKR spoiler + 6 cases of neutralising internal opponents. External instruments — the CEC and Prosecutor General's Office. Click a node for case details.

External spoiler — 'Communists of Russia' (KPKR)

External spoiler — the 'Communist Party Communists of Russia' (KPKR), registered by the Ministry of Justice on 7 June 2012 in the first wave following the liberalisation of the party law, with CC Chairman Maxim Suraykin until March 2022 (Sergei Malinkovich from March 2022). Suraykin participated in the 2018 presidential elections (0.68%, 499,342 votes) with visual branding and rhetoric close to KPRF. At the 2021 Duma elections on 17–19 September, in 7 of the 15 Moscow single-member constituencies, KR fielded candidates with names and surnames identical or near-identical to those of KPRF candidates (in the Lyublinsky constituency — a full namesake of V. Rashkin); Ведомости (08.07.2021) described the 'named spoiler' practice; CEC chair Ella Pamfilova publicly called it "a disgrace", but no spoiler was removed — the law does not prohibit changing one's name and surname through the civil registry.

The speed of KPKR's registration in the first wave after the 2012 party-law liberalisation, and its unobstructed passage through all Ministry of Justice procedures, contrasts with the years-long refusals to register Alexei Navalny's Progress Party, PARNAS, and other non-systemic opposition projects. In March 2022 Suraykin was removed by his party's own congress; the formal change of leadership was approved by the Ministry of Justice without delay — even the spoiler does not remain autonomous.

The case of Pavel Grudinin — the best KPRF candidate of the post-Soviet era

The case of Pavel Grudinin — the best KPRF candidate of the post-Soviet era (11.77% in the 2018 presidential elections). By CEC Resolution No. 30/258-8 of 24 July 2021, on the basis of a letter from his ex-wife and information from the Prosecutor General's Office concerning his ownership of 16,667 shares in the offshore company Bontro LTD (Belize), Grudinin was excluded from KPRF's federal list two months before the Duma elections. KPRF did not mount active public opposition to the CEC — which is itself a behavioural fact. The Lenin State Farm (Sovkhoz im. Lenina) of Grudinin, until 2018 a consistent donor to the party, ceased its transfers after the exclusion.

In 2018 the same information about the Belizean offshore did not prevent him from standing as a presidential candidate — selective application of the exclusion mechanisms. Grudinin publicly disputed the circumstances, claiming that the shares in question had been registered without his knowledge and belonged to his ex-wife as part of a matrimonial property dispute. However, the CEC adopted its resolution on exclusion.

The case of Valery Rashkin — KPRF's 'street' leader

The case of Valery Rashkin (Валерий Рашкин) — KPRF's main 'street' leader, organiser of protest actions after the 2021 Duma elections. On 28 October 2021 in the Lysogorsky district of Saratov Oblast, the carcass of an elk was found in the boot of Rashkin's car; police opened a criminal case under Article 258 of the Criminal Code (illegal hunting). On 25 November 2021 the Duma stripped Rashkin of parliamentary immunity (343/49). On 22 April 2022 the Kirovsky District Court of Saratov sentenced him to 3 years' suspended imprisonment. On 25 May 2022 the State Duma revoked Rashkin's mandate (305/68/3); the KPRF faction voted 'against', but took no independent public action in his defence.

The speed with which the criminal case was opened (within hours of the car being stopped), the speed of the immunity-stripping, and of the final mandate revocation — are atypical for routine illegal-hunting cases. Rashkin is the first KPRF 'street' leader to have his mandate removed during Zyuganov's lifetime.

The case of Sergei Levchenko — pressure through his son

The case of Sergei Levchenko — Governor of Irkutsk Oblast from 18 September 2015, the first elected Communist governor and one of the very few KPRF governors. Irkutsk Oblast is the only region with consistent KPRF wins in regional elections. On 12 December 2019 Levchenko submitted his resignation "of his own accord" eighteen months before the end of his term; simultaneously, a criminal case was under way against his son, Andrei Levchenko, a deputy of the Irkutsk Regional Assembly, accused of embezzling 185 m ₽ from the Regional Capital Repair Fund. On 28 July 2022 the Kuibyshevsky District Court of Irkutsk sentenced Andrei Levchenko to 9 years in a general-regime correctional colony — the cleanest example of systemic pressure on a family to neutralise a regionally successful Communist.

KPRF did not exit the system as a protest following Levchenko's resignation; the party publicly objected to the prosecution of Andrei but did not make it a central issue. In the subsequent gubernatorial election in Irkutsk Oblast in 2020, KPRF fielded Mikhail Shchapov, who received 25.6% (second place) — but that campaign did not generate the same federal resonance as Levchenko's 2015 campaign.

Seleznyov, Semigin, Bondarenko — other neutralisation cases

Additional cases: Gennady Seleznyov — Speaker of the 3rd Duma, expelled from KPRF on 25 May 2002 after refusing to resign his post; Gennady Semigin — the party's main financial donor 1996–2004, expelled on 18 May 2004 "for factionalism", moved to "Patriots of Russia", returned to the Duma in 2021 via SRZP; Nikolai Bondarenko — deputy of the Saratov Regional Assembly and YouTube blogger with an audience of over 1 million subscribers, stripped of his mandate on 28 February 2022 (on the fourth day after the start of the special military operation) on the grounds of an "incomplete declaration" (YouTube donations as income).

All six cases share a common structure: the emergence of a figure with independent electoral potential — a public conflict or electoral success outside the central apparatus's control — a neutralisation mechanism (expulsion, CEC resolution, criminal case, pressure on family, mandate revocation) — the passive-cooperative role of KPRF itself.

Connection to the financial model

KPRF's financial model — near-total dependence on the state budget (84–94% of revenues) — materially explains why the party does not block the mechanisms for neutralising its own opponents. The party's electoral result must remain sufficient to preserve its status as a recipient of state-budget financing (≈1.6 bn ₽/year); this creates an upper ceiling on permissible public opposition. Full-scale blocking of the neutralisation of Grudinin, Rashkin, or Levchenko would mean confrontation with the same institutions (CEC, Prosecutor General's Office, Presidential Administration) that control the electoral process and the distribution of state-budget financing.

The external spoiler limits the growth of the party's electoral result in single-member constituencies; the internal neutralisation limits the growth of politically dangerous leaders capable of becoming candidates in federal presidential elections. Together they form a system in which KPRF stably occupies the position of 'second party' while being guaranteed safe for the system as a whole.

G · Crises and war

Behaviour in the crises of 2022–2024: institutional loyalty to the military-foreign-policy line

In the crisis moments of 2022–2024, the KPRF faction systematically demonstrated institutional support for the military-foreign-policy line. The statement by the KPRF CC Presidium of 24 February 2022 was formulated as anti-Western, not anti-war: the key theses — support for recognition of the DNR/LNR, placing responsibility on "the collective West", a call to "protect the Russian population of Donbas"; the phrases "ceasefire", "negotiations", "civilian casualties" are absent. The active authorial role of KPRF in the political act of recognition: on 15 February 2022, nine days before the start of the war, it was specifically the KPRF bill (introduced by Zyuganov and Kalashnikov) that was adopted, not the alternative bill from United Russia.

Two lanes: MILITARY-REPRESSIVE (KPRF — co-author and supporting party) and SOCIO-ECONOMIC OPPOSITION (genuine opposition to the budget). Red dashed arrow connects ФЗ-32 of 04.03.2022 and Hero of Labour of 26.06.2024 — the functional division.

24.02.2022 — CC Presidium statement: anti-Western, not anti-war

On 24 February 2022 the Presidium of the KPRF Central Committee published a statement on the situation in and around Ukraine. The statement was formulated as anti-Western, not anti-war: the key theses — support for recognition of the DNR and LNR ("a strategically correct step"), placing responsibility for the escalation on "the collective West", NATO, and "the Kyiv regime"; a call to "protect the Russian population of Donbas". The phrases "ceasefire", "negotiations", "troop withdrawal", "civilian casualties" are absent. Compared to the anti-war statement of the Yabloko Federal Political Committee on the same day ("This war is a most heinous crime"), the KPRF statement structurally supports state action.

The 2022 timeline of support — from recognition to annexation

22 February 2022 — ratification of treaties with the DNR/LNR: 400 for, 0 against, 0 abstained. Zyuganov and S. Obukhov were absent from the chamber per the official account (ill with COVID), but appear in the protocol as 'for'. 4 March 2022 — ФЗ-32 on false information about the army: 401/0/0; the KPRF faction voted in favour en bloc; Sinelshchikov defended it from the floor. 20 September 2022 — mobilisation amendments to the Criminal Code (ФЗ-365): 389/0/0; the KPRF faction voted in favour en bloc; no one left the chamber, no one claimed a technical error. 3 October 2022 — annexation of 4 regions: 412/0/0; Afonin from the floor: "Confidently, unanimously, and with great hope and joy, we shall adopt this decision." KPRF deputy Oleg Smolin (visually impaired) accidentally pressed 'against'; the vote was re-cast to record unanimity.

The 2024 migration packages after Crocus City Hall

On 22 March 2024 a terrorist attack took place at the Crocus City Hall concert venue; more than 140 people were killed. Following the attack, over the course of 2024 the State Duma passed at least 21 laws in the field of migration (per Volodin's statement): ФЗ No. 260-FZ of 08.08.2024 (deportation regime + register of supervised persons); ФЗ No. 387-FZ + package of 29.10.2024 (organisation of illegal migration within an organised criminal group as aggravated — confiscation + website blocking); ban on enrolling in schools children of migrants without Russian-language competency from 11.12.2024 (409/1/0); law on digital control of migrants in Moscow and Moscow Oblast from 20.05.2025 (340/0/22).

All 2024 migration laws were supported unanimously by the KPRF faction (deputies from all five factions co-authored them). Mikhail Matveyev (KPRF) voiced critical remarks on the digital control of migrants on 20.05.2025 and did not vote. KPRF, historically declaring "friendship of peoples" in the Soviet tradition, votes for a repressive migration regime.

The 2024 presidential campaign — the culmination of the neutralisation cycle

The 2024 presidential campaign — the culmination of the neutralisation cycle. At the 19th (extraordinary) Congress on 23 December 2023, KPRF nominated Nikolai Kharitonov (75 years old, State Duma deputy since 1993), declining to nominate such potential candidates as Grudinin (expelled in 2021), Levchenko (removed from the governorship in 2019, son's case), or Rashkin (mandate stripped in 2022). The campaign was run with minimal public effort: the fund budget under 200 m ₽ against a permissible ceiling of 400 m ₽. Result — 4.31% (3,768,470 votes), KPRF's worst presidential result in 25 years; simultaneously — ≈407 m ₽ net profit per Forbes calculations, the best financial figure among parliamentary parties.

The coincidence of the neutralisation of the last three potentially electorally attractive party figures in 2019–2022 with the start of the 2023–2024 election cycle is structurally significant. The 'worst electoral result — most profitable campaign' paradox is characteristic of KPRF in 2024. Minimising public activity and the risk of heightened electoral mobilisation allowed the party to emerge from the campaign in financial profit.

Main conclusion for section G

Main conclusion: at moments of crisis, the party chooses institutional loyalty to the state course while maintaining rhetorical criticism of socio-economic policy. This behavioural pattern is consistent with the party's financial model (84–94% state-budget financing), the leader's status (member of Russia's State Council, Hero of Labour), family integration into the executive branch, and the industry of neutralising internal opponents. KPRF is not a 'spoiler' in the simple sense, but a systemic party with a genuine electorate and a functional division of its opposition role.

H · Foreign ties

Foreign financing — not found

Under Art. 30 of Federal Law No. 95-FZ "On Political Parties", foreign donations to Russian parties are explicitly prohibited. In the public grants databases of the National Endowment for Democracy (NED), Open Society Foundations, Heinrich Böll Stiftung, Friedrich Ebert Stiftung, Friedrich Naumann Stiftung, MacArthur, Ford, and Carnegie, no data on financing of KPRF has been found. KPRF is the only parliamentary faction whose leaders ideologically reject residence abroad; the declarations of the faction's leadership have historically not contained foreign assets or residence permits.

Verified sources

Not found in open-source data: foreign accounts held by faction leadership, incoming international donations to the party, Western sanctions against its leaders, membership in international party federations. Ministry of Justice foreign-agent register — KPRF leadership not listed. Sanctions lists of HM Treasury, OFAC, EU, Canada, Australia — Zyuganov, Afonin, Melnikov, Ostanina not listed. Declarator — declarations of faction leadership: no foreign accounts or overseas real estate declared.

Ideological position

The absence of foreign assets among KPRF leadership is a consistent ideological position, distinguishing the party from LDPR (Igor Lebedev, Zhirinovsky's son, who changed his name and moved abroad in 2021) and from a number of United Russia deputies. In this sense, KPRF is the only parliamentary party whose leadership publicly declares 'non-expatriate' loyalty to the country.
