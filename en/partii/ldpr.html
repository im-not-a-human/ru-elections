LDPR — the party after
Zhirinovsky.

Vladimir Zhirinovsky (Владимир Жириновский) — despite its name, an ultra-nationalist faction founder — led LDPR for 33 years before his death on 6 April 2022. Six weeks later, at the extraordinary 34th Congress on 27 May 2022, Leonid Eduardovich Slutsky (Леонид Эдуардович Слуцкий) was elected LDPR chairman unanimously and without any alternative candidate — chairman of the Duma Committee on International Affairs since 2016; in December 2022 he was granted the rank of Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the Russian Federation. The principal public face of the faction on security matters is Andrei Konstantinovich Lugovoy (Андрей Константинович Луговой), co-author of ФЗ № 255-FZ of 14.07.2022 on the control of the activities of persons under foreign influence; the same Lugovoy whom the report of Sir Robert Owen (21.01.2016) found to have placed polonium-210 in the teapot at the Pine Bar on 1 November 2006 — and who remains under HM Treasury and OFAC sanctions to this day.

A · Origins

The first party formally opposing the CPSU before the Soviet collapse — and a personal-brand party from 1991 to 2022.

LDPR's founding chronology begins on 13 December 1989 — the first meeting of the initiative group to create the Liberal Democratic Party of the Soviet Union (LDPSU) in Moscow. On 31 March 1990, at the I. V. Rusakova Cultural Centre, the founding congress was held: 215 delegates from 41 regions; Zhirinovsky was elected chairman. On 12 April 1991 the USSR Ministry of Justice registered the LDPSU — the only registered political party formally opposing the CPSU before the collapse of the Soviet Union.

Founding chronology 1989–1991

LDPR's founding chronology begins on 13 December 1989 — the first meeting of the initiative group to create the Liberal Democratic Party of the Soviet Union (LDPSU) in Moscow, attended by V. Zhirinovsky, V. Bogachyov, M. Dunts, A. Khalitov and others. On 31 March 1990, at the I. V. Rusakova Cultural Centre, the founding congress of the LDPSU was held: 215 delegates from 41 regions; Vladimir Volfovich Zhirinovsky (Владимир Вольфович Жириновский) was elected chairman (surname at birth — Eidenstein, changed in 1964 to his mother's surname). On 12 April 1991 the USSR Ministry of Justice registered the LDPSU — the only registered political party formally opposing the CPSU before the collapse of the Soviet Union. After the August coup the party was reconstituted as the Liberal Democratic Party of Russia (LDPR, rebranded 14 December 1992); under the requirements of ФЗ № 95-FZ "On Political Parties" of 11 July 2001 it was re-registered in 2002.

Journalistic reconstruction of the KGB's role

The most thoroughly documented journalistic account of the role of the Soviet security services in founding the LDPSU is the memoir Sumerki (Twilight) by Alexander Nikolayevich Yakovlev (Александр Николаевич Яковлев) (Materik publishing house, 2003), the chief ideologist of Perestroika and a member of the CPSU Politburo 1987–1990. According to Yakovlev, the party's name was devised by First Deputy KGB Chairman General Filipp Bobkov (head of the 5th Directorate — political counter-intelligence); the CPSU Central Committee's Affairs Department allocated 3 million roubles for founding expenses via Andrei Zavidy, a Zhirinovsky associate in the founding campaign. Bobkov (died 2019) publicly denied any involvement by the 5th Directorate during his lifetime; Zhirinovsky categorically denied any KGB connection throughout his life. In 2021, State Duma deputy Alexander Khinshtein (Александр Хинштейн) published on his Telegram channel a letter from Zhirinovsky to the management of the I. V. Rusakova Cultural Centre (1989), in which it states: "the head of the district KGB department /comrade L. N. Sopelkin/ and the leadership of the Sokolnichesky district CPSU committee have been informed." Direct archival documents from the KGB's 5th Directorate or the Central Committee Affairs Department are not publicly available as of 2026.

Personal-brand party 1991–2022

From registration in 1991 until Zhirinovsky's death on 06.04.2022, LDPR functioned as a personal-brand party: Zhirinovsky was the sole media channel, the sole voice, the sole stylistic identity; delegation of public profile to other party figures was minimal. This structurally distinguishes LDPR from the CPRF (vertical but collegial), Yabloko (with three leadership changes) and SRZP (with several leaders). Electoral trajectory: peak 1993 — 22.92% and 64 seats, first place in the national party-list vote; steady decline from 2011: 11.68% (2011) → 13.14% (2016, local peak) → 7.55% (2021). In the 2024 presidential election Slutsky received 3.20% — close to Zhirinovsky's historical low (2.70% in 2000) and below all his other results, confirming that a personal-brand party does not preserve its mobilisation capacity after the departure of the central figure.

Uncontested succession on 27 May 2022

After Zhirinovsky's death from COVID-19 complications, the party chose the only path of institutional continuity: at the extraordinary 34th Congress on 27 May 2022, Leonid Slutsky was elected chairman unanimously and without any alternative candidate. The uncontested scenario admitted no competing candidates within the party — not Zhirinovsky's elder son Igor Lebedev (LDPR deputy speaker 2011–2021), nor Yaroslav Nilov (Ярослав Нилов), nor any other public faction deputies. This is a structural signal: the party does not treat an alternative leadership scenario as real. In LDPR's functional profile a shift occurred — from 'loud patriotic faction' with ritual oppositional gestures to 'functional majority partner', in which Lugovoy moves into the role of author of the foreign-agent package and Slutsky receives an ambassadorial rank and becomes co-author of mobilisation and gender norms.

B · Financing

≈90% of the budget 2017–2025: 152 ₽ × 4.2 mn votes = ≈646 mn ₽/year.

LDPR is one of two parties among the parliamentary five (the other being the CPRF) with the highest share of state budget financing among opposition parties. Per aggregated data from the CEC's 2024 report as published by Kommersant: 2017 — ≈97%, 2018 — ≈90%, 2022 — ≈87% (≈741 mn ₽), 2023 — ≈90% (≈744 mn ₽), 2024 — 89.4%. The calculation base is the rate under Article 33 of ФЗ-95: 152 ₽ × 4,252,252 votes = ≈646 mn ₽/year in annual state receipts until the next Duma elections.

Rate under Article 33 of ФЗ-95 and the calculation base

LDPR is one of two parties among the parliamentary five (the other being the CPRF) with the highest share of state budget financing among opposition parties. Per aggregated data from the CEC's 2024 report as published by Kommersant and the summary tables of Golos and Vedomosti: 2017 — ≈97%, 2018 — ≈90%, 2022 — ≈87% (≈741 mn ₽), 2023 — ≈90% (≈744 mn ₽), 2024 — 89.4%. The calculation base is the rate under Article 33 of ФЗ-95: 152 ₽ × 4,252,252 votes = ≈646 mn ₽/year in annual state receipts until the next Duma elections. This explains why total party revenues in 2022–2025 hover around 740–770 mn ₽: between elections the primary income source is precisely the ФЗ-95 rate.

A 38% budget reduction over ten years

Over ten years — since 2014 — LDPR's budget has shrunk by ≈38% in proportion to the decline in votes at federal elections: the estimated 2014 budget was ≈1.3 bn ₽ (on the 2011 Duma result of 11.68% and ≈8.7 mn votes) versus ≈800 mn ₽ in 2024. Structurally this confirms that the party receives money from the state in exchange for electoral results recognised by the state, not on the basis of a mass membership or an independent donor market. In 2023, 60% of party funds (445 mn ₽) went on maintaining governing bodies — the central apparatus and federal leadership; only 19% went to local branches. United Russia's share of spending on governing bodies is around 30%; the CPRF's around 45%; LDPR demonstrates the most centralised expenditure structure among parliamentary parties.

The closed loop of the 2024 presidential election

The 2024 presidential campaign illustrated the closed loop. Slutsky's campaign fund as LDPR's candidate was ≈220 mn ₽ against a permitted ceiling of 400 mn ₽; the source was primarily party funds and the federal budget, reinvested via the 152 ₽ mechanism. The result: 3.20% (≈2.8 mn votes), 4th place, below Zhirinovsky's historical low. At the same time, Forbes (March 2024) calculated the party's net profit from the presidential campaign at ≈220 mn ₽ — between the CPRF (≈407 mn) and New People (≈150 mn). The party has no large independent corporate donors: unlike United Russia (substantial corporate donations from state-linked structures), SRZP ('RUSS-INVEST', Gartung's ChKPZ) or New People (Kapitany programme alumni), LDPR does not mobilise donations as a resource — the party functions as a node for distributing state funds, not as a corporate-lobbying agent.

C · Leaders

Slutsky, Lugovoy and the 'absorbing' party for Zhuravlev, Babkin and Degtyarev.

After 06.04.2022 the party is organised around two figures. The chairman — Leonid Slutsky: ambassadorial rank from December 2022, chairmanship of the Duma Committee on International Affairs since 2016, the 2018 harassment case that prevented none of these appointments. The principal public face of the security-policy circuit — Andrei Lugovoy: FSO 1991–1996, named figure in the British public inquiry on the Litvinenko case (polonium-210, 2006), under HM Treasury and OFAC sanctions, yet co-author of ФЗ-255 on 'persons under foreign influence'.

Leonid Slutsky — ambassadorial rank and the Committee on International Affairs

Leonid Eduardovich Slutsky (Леонид Эдуардович Слуцкий) (b. 04.01.1968, Moscow) — State Duma deputy for six consecutive convocations since 1999; profile at duma.gov.ru. From 5 October 2016 — chairman of the Duma Committee on International Affairs (re-elected 12.10.2021). From 27 May 2022 — LDPR chairman, elected at the 34th Congress unanimously and without any alternative candidate. From December 2022 — rank of Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the Russian Federation (the highest diplomatic rank; conferred by presidential authority); Slutsky is the only leader of a parliamentary party in 2022–2026 to hold an active diplomatic rank. Banking career 1994–1999: chairman of the board of directors of Prominvestbank (1994–1997), deputy chairman of Unikombank (1997–1999, the bank served Russia's Ministry of Defence). From 1999 he has worked formally in the capacity of a state parliamentary deputy. Per his 2024 declaration, income ≈14.4 mn ₽; real estate in Moscow and the Moscow Region; no foreign accounts or assets documented.

The 2018 harassment case: the ethics commission found no violations

The 2018 harassment case is one of the most thoroughly documented public crises in Russian parliamentary politics. On 6 March 2018, Farida Rustamova (BBC News Russian Service) published an audio recording of a conversation with Slutsky dated 24 March 2017, in which, as the journalist described, the deputy "ran his hand, palm inward, across my groin upward"; open accusations were also levelled by Yekaterina Kotrikadze (RTVI), Darya Zhuk (Dozhd), and Yelizaveta Antonova. On 21 March 2018, the State Duma ethics commission under the chairmanship of Otari Arshba, sitting in closed session, examined the complaints of three journalists and found no violations on the grounds of "insufficient evidence". Dozhd, RTVI, Echo of Moscow, and Znak.com suspended their work with the State Duma; RBC and Kommersant recalled their correspondents. By the time of Slutsky's election as party chairman (27.05.2022) the scandal was not mentioned in LDPR's public rhetoric; no intraparty ethical reassessment took place.

Andrei Lugovoy — FSO, ORT and the Litvinenko case

Andrei Konstantinovich Lugovoy (Андрей Константинович Луговой) (b. 19.09.1966, Baku) — State Duma deputy on the LDPR list from December 2007 (5th, 6th, 7th, and 8th convocations); first deputy chairman of the Duma Committee on Security and Anti-Corruption in the 7th–8th convocations; profile at duma.gov.ru. Security-service career: Kremlin Regiment → 1991 — transferred to the 1st Department of the Main Guard Directorate of the Russian Federation (now the FSO); 1991–1996 — operational officer, 1st Department of the GUO/FSO: personal protection of senior officials (per published accounts: Yegor Gaidar, Sergei Filatov, Andrei Kozyrev, Boris Berezovsky). 1996–2000 — head of the ORT security service. On 21 January 2016, Sir Robert Owen published the report of the UK public inquiry: "I am sure that Mr Lugovoy and Mr Kovtun placed the polonium-210 in the teapot at the Pine Bar on 1 November 2006. I am also sure that they did this with the intention of poisoning Mr Litvinenko" — the operation was, the report concluded, "probably approved" by FSB Director N. Patrushev and President V. Putin (gov.uk). 21 January 2016 — HM Treasury asset freeze; sanctions renewed in 2018, 2020, 2022. In January 2017 Lugovoy was placed on the US Treasury (OFAC) Magnitsky sanctions list. The Russian Federation has not extradited Lugovoy to the United Kingdom under Article 61 of the Constitution. Lugovoy is co-author of ФЗ № 255-FZ of 14.07.2022 "On the control of the activities of persons under foreign influence"; he presented the bill publicly as a member of the Duma commission on foreign interference and described criticism of the law as "ordinary betrayal".

Other key faction deputies

Yaroslav Yevgenyevich Nilov (Ярослав Евгеньевич Нилов) — chairman of the Duma Committee on Labour, Social Policy and Veterans' Affairs since 2016; deputy since 2011; first cousin of Oleg Nilov of A Just Russia — For Truth; votes in line with the faction in all key divisions. Sergei Alexandrovich Karginov (Сергей Александрович Каргинов) — State Duma deputy since 2007; deputy chairman of the Duma Committee on Budget and Taxes. Alexei Ivanovich Zhuravlev (Алексей Иванович Журавлёв) — the only deputy of the Rodina party in the 8th convocation; joined the LDPR faction in 2021; first deputy chairman of the Duma Defence Committee; author of a 2013 bill to strip homosexuals of parental rights; in December 2023 proposed blocking ≈20 emoji as "propagandising non-traditional values".

Degtyarev and Babkin — integration trajectories

Mikhail Vladimirovich Degtyarev (Михаил Владимирович Дегтярёв) (b. 10.07.1981) — State Duma deputy for LDPR in the 5th–7th convocations (2011–2021); from 20 July 2020 — acting Governor of Khabarovsk Krai (by presidential decree following the detention and arrest of Sergei Furgal; re-elected in September 2021), served until May 2024. On 14 May 2024 appointed Minister of Sport of the Russian Federation in the new government of M. Mishustin (government.ru) — the first LDPR deputy to move from a regional governorship to a federal ministerial post; the most significant integration trajectory of an LDPR functionary into the executive hierarchy in the party's history. Konstantin Anatolyevich Babkin (Константин Анатольевич Бабкин) (co-owner of Rostselmash) joined LDPR in December 2024 after the Supreme Court liquidated the Party of Business on 27.11.2024; he became chairman of the party's economic council and joined the supreme council (Коммерсантъ).

D · State ties

The chairman's diplomatic rank; the security-service background of the foreign-agent law's author; integration into the executive hierarchy through Degtyarev.

LDPR is a parliamentary party whose leaders are structurally integrated into the state circuit. The party chairman received the highest diplomatic rank six months after his election. The party's principal public deputy on security matters — a former FSO officer, subject to the sanctions of two Western governments — is co-author of ФЗ-255 on foreign agents. A former faction deputy was appointed federal Minister of Sport on 14 May 2024 — the first such trajectory in the party's history.

Slutsky's ambassadorial rank (December 2022)

In December 2022 Slutsky was granted the rank of Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the Russian Federation. This is the highest diplomatic rank in the Russian MFA system; conferral is within the President's authority. Slutsky is the only leader of a parliamentary party in 2022–2026 to hold an active diplomatic rank. The decision was taken six months after his election at the 34th LDPR Congress and five months after the passage of ФЗ-255 on foreign agents, co-authored with Lugovoy. Since 2016 Slutsky has been chairman of the Duma Committee on International Affairs — the parliament's key foreign-policy hub, whose operational line is formally coordinated with the Russian MFA. Slutsky is also chairman of the Council of the Imperial Orthodox Palestine Society (IOPS), a historic organisation with a special public-state status whose Council includes representatives of the Presidential Administration and the MFA.

Lugovoy's security-service background and the parliamentary security circuit

Lugovoy's security-service background and his current position in the parliamentary security circuit. Lugovoy is a former FSO officer (1st Department, GUO, 1991–1996), then head of ORT security under B. Berezovsky (1996–2000), a named figure in the British public inquiry on the Litvinenko case, under HM Treasury sanctions since January 2016 and on the US Treasury sanctions list since January 2017. Since 2007 — State Duma deputy; in the 7th–8th convocations — first deputy chairman of the Duma Committee on Security and Anti-Corruption; member of the Duma commission investigating foreign interference. This position provides Lugovoy with functional integration into the legislative formalisation of state security-policy decisions; it also explains his role as author of ФЗ-255 (the unified foreign-agent law of 14.07.2022) and the extensions of the foreign-agent regime in 2024–2026 (including ФЗ-414 of December 2024 on rouble special-purpose accounts for foreign agents). Structurally this creates a significant public contradiction: Russian legislation on 'foreign influence' is produced by a figure whose biography is directly connected with episodes that formed the basis for international sanctions.

Degtyarev: faction → governor → minister (2020 → 2024)

Institutional integration into the executive hierarchy through M. Degtyarev. Mikhail Degtyarev — LDPR State Duma deputy in the 5th–7th convocations; in July 2020 appointed by the President as acting Governor of Khabarovsk Krai following the detention of Sergei Furgal; re-elected in September 2021; in May 2024 appointed Minister of Sport of the Russian Federation in M. Mishustin's new government. This is the only 'faction deputy → regional governor → federal minister' trajectory in LDPR's history; among parliamentary 'opposition' parties in 2022–2026 no comparable trajectories are recorded in open sources. The very fact of his party affiliation being retained upon federal ministerial appointment (under ФЗ № 273-FZ government members are not required to leave their party) establishes Degtyarev's position as simultaneously a parallel state functionary and a party figure. Party leaders' awards: Zhirinovsky received the Order for Services to the Fatherland, 1st (2016) and 2nd (2011) class, the Order of Alexander Nevsky (2014), the Order of Friendship (2007), and the title of Honoured Lawyer of the Russian Federation (2002) over his tenure. Slutsky holds the Order for Services to the Fatherland, 4th class.

The agreed succession scenario of 27.05.2022

The party's succession after Zhirinovsky's death was assured not by a competitive election but by an agreed scenario. The unanimous uncontested election of Slutsky on 27 May 2022 — six weeks after the death of the party's 33-year chairman — was an institutional procedure that admitted no internal conflict. None of the faction's public deputies — not Zhirinovsky's elder son Igor Lebedev (deputy speaker 2011–2021, who according to Baza/Life.ru changed his name to 'David Alexandrovich Garcia' and emigrated as early as 2021), nor Ya. Nilov, nor Lugovoy — put themselves forward as alternative candidates. This scenario coincides with the state's interests: the party does not enter a phase of public intraparty conflict at a crisis moment of war and mobilisation; the successor (Slutsky) is already integrated into the diplomatic vertical through his chairmanship of the Duma Committee on International Affairs.

E · Voting

≈98–100% support 2022–2025; zero systemic 'against' votes; Slutsky and Lugovoy as co-authors.

The aggregate share of support by the LDPR faction for restrictive and military-repressive laws 2022–2025 is ≈98–100%. This is the highest rate among opposition parliamentary factions: CPRF — 83%, SRZP — 89%, New People — 94%. Since 2022 LDPR has not a single 'against' vote on key restrictive laws; the only significant episode of faction-level opposition before 2022 was a vote 'against' the Yarovaya package on 24.06.2016 (together with the CPRF). After Zhirinovsky's death even decorative 'oppositional' gestures are no longer reproduced.

Lugovoy — author of ФЗ-255 on 'persons under foreign influence'

Lugovoy is co-author of ФЗ № 255-FZ of 14.07.2022 "On the control of the activities of persons under foreign influence" (bill No. 113045-8). The law merged three previously separate regimes (foreign media, NGOs, and individuals) into a single framework and extended the 'foreign agent' status to any persons "under foreign influence" — without the mandatory requirement of actual foreign funding. As of November 2025 the register contains more than 800 persons. Lugovoy publicly presented the bill as a member of the Duma commission on foreign interference and described criticism of the law as "ordinary betrayal". The co-authorship by a figure subject to British and American sanctions over the Litvinenko case (polonium-210, 2006) creates a direct structural line: a state actor identified by Western governments as a national-security threat becomes the author of Russia's definition of 'foreign influence'.

Slutsky — co-author of mobilisation, the LGBT ban and the ban on gender reassignment

Slutsky is co-author of the mobilisation amendments (No. 160006-8, ФЗ-365 of 24.09.2022): the law introduces the concepts of 'mobilisation' and 'martial law', toughens penalties for desertion, voluntary surrender (up to 10 years), and looting (up to 15 years). Third-reading vote on 20.09.2022 — unanimous. The law was signed on the day partial mobilisation was announced (21.09.2022). Co-authors were Slutsky (LDPR) and Oleg Nilov (A Just Russia — For Truth) — the only case of joint authorship between two 'opposition' parties on military legislation. Slutsky is also one of 390 co-authors of the law banning LGBT 'propaganda' (No. 217471-8, ФЗ № 478-FZ of 05.12.2022) and of the law banning gender reassignment (No. 376846-8, ФЗ № 386-FZ of 24.07.2023). This transforms Slutsky from a 'voter in favour' to an authorial figure on the cultural-conservative agenda.

The anomaly of 25.07.2023: tactical non-attendance on ФЗ-439

The anomaly of 25 July 2023: ФЗ-439 raising the conscription age to 30. LDPR largely did not vote (the 'non-attendance' tactic). In Duma practice this does not mean public disagreement but tactical evasion of formal accountability: without 'against' votes the law passes on United Russia's majority, but the faction insulates itself from direct association with the unpopular measure of expanding the conscription pool. This is not opposition — it is the neutralisation of political responsibility while maintaining the overall course. LDPR is historically the most anti-migration and most anti-Western party in rhetoric; 'tactical non-attendance' on the conscription age is the only notable episode in which the faction chose not to share responsibility for a specific mobilisation decision, while issuing no public statement against the decision itself. On ФЗ № 281-FZ on VPN/extremism search (No. 755710-8) of 22 July 2025, LDPR voted 'in favour' — unlike the CPRF and New People, who voted 'against'; this is the final signal that after 2022 the digital-policy agenda within the party has ceased to be a zone of even decorative public opposition.

F · Managed opposition

Monopoly on the nationalist-patriotic niche + 'absorbing' Zhuravlev and Babkin.

LDPR is the only parliamentary party in whose entire history the Ministry of Justice has never registered doppelganger parties or parties with similar names. The doppelganger technique, applied to individual opposition politicians and to the CPRF via Suraykin's 'Communists of Russia', is not applied to LDPR. This is a structural sign that in the party system LDPR is required as the monopoly holder of the nationalist-patriotic electoral segment, not as an object of weakening.

Monopoly on the electoral segment

LDPR is the only parliamentary party in whose entire history the Ministry of Justice has never registered doppelganger parties or parties with similar names. The doppelganger technique, applied to individual opposition politicians and to the CPRF via Suraykin's 'Communists of Russia', is not applied to LDPR. This is a structural sign that in the party system LDPR is required as the monopoly holder of the nationalist-patriotic electoral segment, not as an object of weakening. Any attempts to create competing projects in the niche of 'Russian nationalism + patriotism' — nationalist-patriotic 'pro-war special-operation parties' and the like — had not progressed beyond founding meetings by 2026.

The Rodina case: Zhuravlev as a technical faction member

A unique function of LDPR in the party system of 2024–2026 is its role as an 'absorbing' party for leaders of liquidated affiliated projects. Case 1: the Rodina party (registered by the Ministry of Justice 21.12.2012; leader — Alexei Zhuravlev), at the 2021 elections — 0.8% (below the threshold for state funding). In 2021 the party's sole deputy, Zhuravlev, joined the LDPR faction; he serves as first deputy chairman of the Duma Defence Committee and votes in line with the LDPR faction. Rodina retains its brand and legal entity, but its only deputy uses the resources of the LDPR faction. This is a technical integration of two parties occupying a close ideological niche, expanding the LDPR faction to 22 seats in the 8th Duma (plus Zhuravlev's one seat).

The Party of Business case: Babkin and industrial lobbying

Case 2: the Party of Business (Konstantin Babkin, co-owner of Rostselmash). The party was suspended by the Ministry of Justice on 22.04.2024 and liquidated by the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation on 27.11.2024 (RIA). In December 2024 Babkin joined LDPR, became chairman of its economic council and joined the supreme council (Коммерсантъ). Structural significance: LDPR is expanding its functional profile into the industrial-lobbying sphere; Babkin is the public face of agricultural-subsidy lobbying (programme 1432, ≈34 bn ₽ in 2014–2018, with 78% redistributed between two players including Rostselmash), and within LDPR continues this function through the 'economic council'. Counter-example: Boris Titov's Party of Growth joined New People in April 2024, not LDPR, showing that LDPR's 'absorbing' function operates only for projects with a close nationalist-patriotic or industrial-patriotic niche.

Faction cohesion 2021–2026

Internal faction dynamics in 2021–2026 are the most cohesive among opposition factions. On all key votes in 2022–2025 the LDPR faction votes unanimously. Instances of individual dissent — as seen in the CPRF (Shargunov, Matveyev, Kurinny), New People (Avksentyeva, Goryacheva), and SRZP (Kuznetsov with the 'pressed the wrong button' episode) — are not recorded in LDPR. The handover of power from Zhirinovsky to Slutsky proceeded via an unanimous uncontested scenario; the inheritance disputes among the heirs (Igor Lebedev / 'David Alexandrovich Garcia' against the illegitimate son Oleg Eidenstein) are private, not party, matters. The party has no public dissidents and has not lost any significant deputies from the faction at crisis moments — which sets it apart even from SRZP.

G · Crises and war

The Zhirinovsky era → the Slutsky era: 51 days between death and unanimous election.

At the crisis moments of 2022–2026 LDPR demonstrates consistent and monolithic support for the government's course. The party issues no public statements against the war, mobilisation, annexation, or migration restrictions; the party's deputies — Slutsky and Lugovoy — are co-authors of key restrictive norms. Unlike Yabloko (anti-war position), the CPRF (ambivalent — supporting the war while posturing on social policy) or New People (mild posturing on digital freedoms), LDPR has not a single public episode of dissent or disagreement with the course of 2022–2026. This makes the party structurally the most integrated into the state system of all parliamentary opposition parties. Slutsky publicly interpreted the ratification of the annexation of 4 regions on 03.10.2022 as "Zhirinovsky's triumph" — the realisation of the party founder's historical slogans about "returning historically Russian territories".

Two eras of the party: Zhirinovsky (1991–06.04.2022) and Slutsky (from 27.05.2022). A red dashed arrow connects the death and the unanimous election — 51 days.

February–March 2022: recognition of the DNR/LNR; ФЗ-32 on 'false information'

Zhirinovsky supported the President's decision to launch the 'special military operation' with the rhetoric of 'the West betrayed us': the party historically contained elements of foreign-policy criticism of the West, and in February 2022 Zhirinovsky characterised the war in public statements as a response to "NATO's long-term expansion". LDPR voted 'in favour' on the recognition of the DNR/LNR on 22.02.2022 (together with all parliamentary factions, including the CPRF as the initiator). On ФЗ-32 on 'false information about the army' of 04.03.2022 — 'in favour' with the full faction (full Duma result — 401/0/0, unanimous).

06.04.2022 → 27.05.2022: 51 days of succession

After Zhirinovsky's death on 06.04.2022 — condolences from the President and other institutional figures; the party maintains its line of support for the government's course. Fifty-one days later — at the extraordinary 34th Congress on 27 May 2022 — Leonid Slutsky was elected LDPR chairman unanimously and without any alternative candidate. The scenario admitted no competing candidates within the party — not Igor Lebedev (Zhirinovsky's elder son, deputy speaker 2011–2021), nor Ya. Nilov, nor Lugovoy. This is a structural signal: the party does not enter a phase of public intraparty conflict at a crisis moment of war and mobilisation; the successor is already integrated into the diplomatic vertical through his chairmanship of the Duma Committee on International Affairs since 2016.

14.07.2022 ФЗ-255 (Lugovoy) → 20.09.2022 mobilisation (Slutsky) → 03.10.2022 annexation

Co-authors of legislation on 'foreign influence' and mobilisation. 14.07.2022 — ФЗ-255, the unified foreign-agent law, is passed; Lugovoy — co-author. 20.09.2022 — third reading of ФЗ-365 on mobilisation (Articles 207.3, 280.3, 352.1, 356.1 of the Criminal Code); Slutsky — co-author of the second-reading amendments together with Oleg Nilov (A Just Russia — For Truth). Vote unanimous across all five factions. The law was signed on the day partial mobilisation was announced (21.09.2022). 03.10.2022 — ratification of the annexation of 4 regions (DNR 413, LNR 412, Zaporizhzhia 409, Kherson 411). Slutsky addressed the Duma welcoming the decision, using the formula "Zhirinovsky's triumph" — publicly interpreting the annexation as the realisation of the party founder's historical slogans.

December 2022: ambassadorial rank for Slutsky

Six months after his election at the 34th LDPR Congress and five months after the passage of ФЗ-255, co-authored with Lugovoy, Slutsky was granted the rank of Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the Russian Federation. This is the highest diplomatic rank in the Russian MFA system; conferral is within the President's authority. Slutsky is the only leader of a parliamentary party in 2022–2026 with an active diplomatic rank.

2024 presidential election: 3.20%, fourth place

The 2024 presidential campaign became the culmination of the party's electoral decline after Zhirinovsky. Slutsky was nominated by the parliamentary party without collecting signatures; the campaign was built on stylistic imitation of Zhirinovsky — sharp public statements, ritual criticism of the government for 'insufficient firmness', support for the war, annexation, mobilisation, and tougher migration policy. The result: 3.20% (≈2.8 mn votes), 4th place — close to Zhirinovsky's historical low (2.70% in 2000), below all his other results, below LDPR's 2021 Duma result (7.55%) and below New People's Davankov (3.85%). The party after Zhirinovsky has not preserved its mobilisation capacity in federal presidential elections. Concurrently, Lugovoy is co-author of ФЗ-414 of December 2024 on rouble special-purpose accounts for foreign agents and a series of targeted extensions of the foreign-agent regime in 2025; the faction's vote — unanimously 'in favour'.

Final conclusion: a functional appendage of the decision-making centre

The main conclusion: LDPR after Zhirinovsky is a functional appendage of the decision-making centre. The party chairman (Slutsky) holds an ambassadorial rank and chairs the key Duma committee on international affairs; the party's principal public deputy (Lugovoy) is co-author of the foreign-agent legislation and subject to international sanctions; deputies absorbed from liquidated projects (Babkin from the Party of Business, Zhuravlev from Rodina) occupy functional niches in the party structure; a former faction deputy (Degtyarev) was transferred to the post of federal Minister of Sport in May 2024. The biographical profiles of the party's leaders and the behavioural pattern of the faction in 2022–2026 are incompatible with the hypothesis that LDPR is a political force autonomous from the state.

H · Foreign ties

No foreign funding identified; Lugovoy and Slutsky subject to Western government sanctions.

Under Article 30 of ФЗ № 95-FZ "On Political Parties", foreign donations to Russian parties are explicitly prohibited. In the public grant databases of the National Endowment for Democracy (NED), Open Society Foundations, Heinrich Böll Stiftung, Friedrich Ebert Stiftung, Friedrich Naumann Stiftung, MacArthur, Ford, and Carnegie there is no data on the financing of LDPR. For LDPR — historically positioned as a nationalist-patriotic party with strong anti-Western rhetoric — the absence of foreign financing is structurally expected.

Lugovoy and Slutsky — subjects of foreign sanctions regimes

A distinct matter is Lugovoy as a subject of foreign sanctions regimes. Since 21 January 2016 Lugovoy's assets have been frozen by HM Treasury on the basis of Sir Robert Owen's findings regarding his role in the polonium-210 murder of Litvinenko; sanctions were renewed in 2018, 2020, and 2022. Since January 2017 Lugovoy has been placed on the US Treasury (OFAC) Magnitsky sanctions list. These are foreign sanctions, not foreign financing; technically the matter falls outside the scope of this section, but is recorded here for completeness: the faction's deputy who authors foreign-agent legislation in the Duma is himself subject to the sanctions of two Western governments in connection with the use of a radioactive substance abroad. Slutsky has been under sanctions from the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, Japan, and the European Union since 2022 (following the start of the war). A full review of the foreign ties of all Russian parties and sanctions episodes is in the file 04-foreign-ties.md.
